My Current Worldview

Minimal executable kernel

This kernel states how this working worldview admits claims and changes them before the personal exposition begins. Named scholar–concept pairs cue relevant arguments and alternatives; the explicit operation and its checked grounds govern the commitment.

  1. Type the commitment. Charles S. Peirce: fallibilism. Separate observation, definition, model, conditional proof, value, and ontological interpretation. State target, boundary, timescale, source/version, uncertainty, serious rivals, and a revision condition; preserve unknown fields.

  2. Stabilize the target. Rudolf Carnap: explication. Specify the intended sense of life, consciousness, agency, or value before assigning membership. Preserve what a participant reports without treating introspective vividness, thinness, or uncertainty as a complete account of its nature.

  3. Locate the contribution. Daniel C. Dennett: Real Patterns. Compare candidate patterns and boundaries through prediction, perturbation, replacement, recovery, and transfer. Separate the experiencing subject proposed by a theory from causal contribution and cognitive organization.

  4. Model the observation. Judea Pearl: structural causal models. Include elicitation, attention, reporting policy, memory, and environmental dependencies in the observation process. Track changes caused by inquiry and use time-indexed variables for feedback.

  5. Compare whole alternatives. Charles S. Peirce: abduction. Retain the positive explanatory work and unresolved commitments of each serious account. Conditional derivation, independent causal evidence, coherence, economy, and practical usefulness earn different scoped conclusions.

  6. Justify action. Amartya Sen: the capability approach. Separate factual support, normative premises, protected bearer, authority, and responsibility. Relational moral relevance, direct standing, and phenomenal welfare require their own grounds and proportionate treatment of uncertainty.

  7. Revise and stop. Ronald A. Howard: value of information. Correct only the propositions and support paths actually defeated; preserve independent findings and the exact scope of an interlocutor’s concession. Reopen dependent conclusions and stop when further inquiry has insufficient expected information or decision value. Report unresolved truth separately from provisional rejection of a working ontology and from opposition to its unearned explanatory or decision authority.

A provisional map of reality, God, agency, consciousness, physicalism, and what I think we can actually know.

My starting point is not atheism, physicalism, idealism, religion, mysticism, or even science as a slogan. It is a simpler fact: I never encounter reality from nowhere.

I am a bounded biological organism inside the thing I am trying to understand. My senses sample a narrow range of what exists. My nervous system compresses information. My memory is selective and reconstructive. I inherit language, categories, metaphors, social assumptions, and causal expectations before I am capable of examining most of them. Other people are in the same general position, and so are the institutions we build. Computational systems add different capacities and different failure modes, not a view from outside the universe.

My bounded access warns against assigning high confidence to an exhaustive ontology without a defended bridge. It leaves very high confidence in well-supported local claims available. Astronomy gives us substantial evidence beyond Earth, and universal claims can be warranted without inspecting every case. The relevant questions concern inference, coverage, resolution, and successful transport. I use no literal fraction of cosmic history or spacetime as a Bayesian probability cap.

That means I do not begin with “trust reason,” “trust science,” “trust experience,” “trust tradition,” or “trust the model.”

I begin with a design problem:

Given that every observer and every instrument is limited, how do I build ways for reality, evidence, and serious rivals to expose where my representation is wrong?

That is the role of Sweet Rationalism, the Recursive Corrigibility Framework, the Framework Bridge, and my Provisional Boundary- and Scale-Relative Realism. They are not a creed that tells me what reality must be. They are supposed to make it harder for me to protect a conclusion from the evidence that should change it.

The shortest rule remains:

Do not add what the evidence did not earn. Do not subtract what the evidence did not defeat.

Everything below is a current consequence of applying that rule, not an exemption from it.

I treat inherited categories as transformed evidence, not transparent reality

Cosmic boundedness has a historical and cognitive analogue. I do not encounter most of reality, other people, ancient traditions, scientific constructs, or even my own past directly. I encounter representations that have already passed through perception, memory, language, social categories, recording practices, institutions, archives, translations, interfaces, and selection pressures.

target → lived/practical encoding → linguistic categorization → recording → institutional selection → preservation/destruction → commentary/systematization → translation → disciplinary/model classification → present representation

The present representation is evidence about the target and about that transformation history. It is not automatically identical with either. When a conclusion depends on a category such as person, self, agent, disease, religion, species, intelligence, consciousness, information, race, gender, civilization, or mind, I therefore ask whether the category tracks a robust pattern, a historically local compression, an institutional convenience, or some mixture of these.

This does not make the oldest recoverable layer automatically truer, later development automatically corrupt, or marginalized material automatically correct. Early texts can already be composite; later traditions can discover genuine structure; powerful institutions can preserve true claims; suppressed traditions can contain error. Selection history changes evidential availability, not truth by decree.

For historical and cultural questions I therefore try to reconstruct source language, genre, material practice, archaeological context, competing recensions, transmission, canonization, translation, and what failed to survive. For human claims I treat an utterance as context-sensitive participant evidence rather than a transparent readout of an intrinsic essence. For computational systems I apply the same rule to self-description: base model, runtime context, memory, tools, policies, interfaces, and social scaffolds can all shape the representation produced at a moment.

Before treating a category, text, report, or self-model as reality, reconstruct the transformations and selections that made that representation available; then ask whether decompression changes prediction, intervention, uncertainty, or action.

This is operationalized by the Genealogical Decompression / Selection-Aware operator inside the Recursive Corrigibility Framework, the corresponding transport audit in the Framework Bridge, and the ontological admission constraint in PBSRR. The operator is itself provisional: if the extra genealogy does not materially change a live inference, the correct move is to stop rather than turn historical reconstruction into ritual.

Why an open question can still receive a rejection

I provisionally reject universal felt constitution and causal Platonic ingress as my working ontology on the arguments examined here. Their truth remains unresolved. I have not rejected process, relation, mathematical structure, higher-level causation, unfamiliar agency, or the possibility that a richer account will eventually earn its scope. Nor have I established exhaustive physicalism. Peircean fallibilism requires me to revise when the comparison changes; it does not require me to adopt every live possibility in the meantime.

Levin’s published proposal makes the stakes concrete. It offers nonphysical causal patterns and connects the programme to biology, cognition, and ethics. It also acknowledges conjecture and abandonment conditions. My detailed critique preserves those qualifications while questioning which achievements support the stronger attribution. Useful experiments can remain valuable when the ontology used to motivate or reinterpret them has not earned acceptance.

The practical stance follows from both warrant and consequences. An inadequately supported source claim should not acquire established explanatory status or privileged decision authority merely because its universal falsity has not been proved. Uncritical promotion can narrow inquiry and misallocate attention, protection, or power. Those are risks to examine, not automatic proof of harm by a named person. Premature exclusion of unfamiliar welfare or agency is a competing risk. I therefore retain warranted findings, reject unearned promotion, use proportionate safeguards, and specify what would change my mind. The same rule can reject one of my own preferred claims tomorrow.

I am not simply “an atheist,” “an agnostic,” or “a believer”

Those labels become misleading when “God” is treated as though it were one well-defined hypothesis.

My position depends on what the word is doing.

If someone says God is an interventionist supernatural person who created the world recently, flooded the entire planet within human history, specially created biological species, dictated an empirically inerrant scripture, suspends ordinary causal regularities in publicly detectable ways, or performs specific historical miracles, those are not merely ineffable metaphysical intuitions. They contain empirical and historical commitments.

For claims like those, I can be a strong atheist in the claim-specific sense. Where a supernatural claim is sufficiently clear, makes strong claims about the public world, conflicts with well-established geology, evolutionary biology, archaeology, textual history, or other convergent evidence, or is massively outperformed by naturalistic rivals, I do not think permanent agnosticism is epistemically virtuous. Some claims are disfavored strongly enough to reject.

That does not mean I infer:

One literalist supernatural claim failed, therefore no possible thing anyone could ever call God exists.

That would exceed the evidence in the opposite direction.

For a specified supernatural agent that remains logically coherent but has not generated evidence distinguishing it from lower-commitment naturalistic explanations, I am ordinarily a nonbeliever. I withhold positive ontological credit. Hitchens's burden principle is useful here when applied symmetrically: unsupported assertions do not earn belief, but failure to establish X is not automatically proof of not-X.

My position is claim-specific conceptual and evidential skepticism. I use methodological ignosticism when a divine claim needs clarification before evaluation, and agnosticism when an intelligible claim remains unresolved. “Being,” “love,” or “the ground of possibility” can carry meaningful content; I ask which content and which application are warranted. A role-description can remain intelligible while its occupant is unknown. God-concepts and their documented social effects are real; independently existing divine subjects require separate grounds. A change of meaning across an inference needs repair. SEP’s distinction between local and global God-claims helps keep each judgment attached to its actual target.

For still other claims, especially coherent claims about the intrinsic nature of reality that do not yet discriminate themselves from serious rivals, I am genuinely agnostic. That includes some forms of deism, panentheism, neutral-ground metaphysics, panexperientialism, idealism, and claims about what causal actuality is “in itself.” I do not regard “not currently testable by a simple laboratory experiment” as equivalent to meaningless. I do require the claim to earn whatever strength it asks me to grant it.

So my God-position is not one point on a line between believer and atheist. It is a map:

  • Strong disbelief or rejection for sufficiently specified supernatural claims that are directly contradicted or severely disfavored by the relevant evidence.
  • Nonbelief for specified supernatural additions that have not earned distinctive support.
  • Ignosticism when “God” has not been specified well enough to determine what is being asserted.
  • Agnosticism for coherent metaphysical possibilities where the evidence does not currently discriminate among serious rivals.
  • Realism about gods, religions, scriptures, rituals, institutions, sacred places, myths, denominations, and religious identities as historical and social patterns with genuine causal consequences.

Those positions are not contradictory because they concern different targets.

I think gods are real in one important sense that does not establish supernatural gods

My constructive conjecture: we can build gods that earn their influence

I conjecture that some socially instantiated God-patterns can be developed into corrigible arrangements of memory, attention, coordination, and care. Human participation, living dependencies, and computational support can change what those arrangements can do. I want to investigate whether specific changes improve appropriate assistance, reachable options, and durable correction against strong existing and secular alternatives under comparable resources. Those are prospective claims, not results established by naming the arrangement God.

For God-as-agapē, I interpret love as a practical commitment to notice needs, support agency, organize appropriate assistance, confront harm, and revise failed practices. That ethical interpretation needs reasons; the mechanisms need evidence; the religious framing needs its own justification. People can reject the interpretation and still receive support, challenge the arrangement, or leave. If the grouping or name adds nothing useful, I would retain the work and revise the theology. The larger claim about an independently existing divine subject remains separate.

This is one of the places where ordinary “religion versus atheism” language is especially bad.

YHWH, Jesus, Allah, Krishna, Shiva, Zeus, ancestors, saints, demons, heavens, hells, scriptures, denominations, rituals, pilgrimage routes, churches, mosques, temples, religious law, iconography, and sacred narratives have shaped actual human history.

They alter marriages, wars, architecture, art, voting, diet, law, kinship, education, sexuality, charity, punishment, identity, migration, music, grief, hope, and what people are willing to die for.

Those effects are physically realized. They propagate through nervous systems, speech, writing, buildings, institutions, artifacts, networks, practices, and repeated cultural transmission.

A corporation can be real without being a new elementary particle. English is real without being located in one sound wave. A nation is real without possessing a nation-particle. A hurricane is real even though no individual molecule contains a hurricane.

Social ontology gives us sophisticated ways to describe this kind of reality. Brian Epstein distinguishes the conditions that ground social facts from the processes that anchor those conditions. Francesco Guala develops accounts of historical and multifunctional social kinds. Ara Norenzayan and colleagues study how cognition and cultural transmission generate the recurrence and diversity of religious beliefs. Archaeological and historical work can then trace particular religious lineages and material practices.

So I can say:

A god-concept can be a real historical-social causal pattern without that fact establishing a supernatural referent corresponding to the concept.

That is not a trick designed to call atheism “belief in God.” It is a boundary distinction.

The existence of Christianity is not evidence that the Resurrection occurred. The reality of YHWH as a historically transmitted religious kind does not by itself establish an incorporeal divine person. Likewise, demonstrating that a religion has a cultural history does not prove that every supernatural proposition associated with it is false. That would be the genetic fallacy.

When an experience is offered as evidence of its own external source, its origin becomes relevant to that evidential claim. I ask how the report was produced and whether that process reliably identifies the proposed referent. Independent support can change the comparison. I infer neither illusion from bodily mediation nor divine contact from profound conviction. Where the source remains unresolved, I preserve the experience and its consequences while withholding the stronger identification.

The social realization and the supernatural referent are separate claims.

The constructive theology I am proposing

I naturalize Whitehead's God by investigating warranted functions of inheritance, relevance, aims, and preserved influence through identifiable processes. Perfect cosmic retention, primordial valuation, and universal initial aims remain claims under examination. A function being assigned to God within a scheme does not establish that reality requires it.

I recognize God-concepts as real, causally consequential social constructs. Particular influences need evidence, and a larger God-pattern needs explanatory leverage beyond its component practices. Their influence establishes neither an independently existing divine subject nor automatic benevolence, personhood, or consciousness. My God-as-agapē interpretation concerns care enacted through participation; its ethical warrant and the success of particular practices remain criticizable.

Participation is necessary for a participatory care practice to occur. Particular coordination may be necessary in one arrangement, with replacements still to examine. Religious belief and the God-name acquire no monopoly on care. People can refuse my interpretation, leave a community, or decline forgiveness while remaining entitled to justified assistance and protection.

What I now think explains the pattern connecting these discoveries

The strongest provisional conclusion from these explorations is more definite than a generic statement that everything is connected. I think recurring organizations are best explained, at the domains examined so far, by shared physical and mathematical constraints, convergent processes, and historical continuity. An additional common source remains possible, but its necessity or identity has not been established. These explanations can coexist. Similar forms can be produced independently under comparable conditions, then preserved through ancestry, memory, selection, or culture.

This matters because the cases are not merely metaphors for one another. Organic molecules form in extraterrestrial environments; Bennu samples contain a diverse inventory of organic compounds, with a tentative tryptophan identification. Tryptophan is connected through known terrestrial biochemical pathways to tryptamine and, under appropriate conditions, DMT. Those are chemical relationships. They are not evidence that DMT was discovered in space, that extraterrestrial material entered this exact biological lineage, or that psychedelic experience connects a person to an extraterrestrial informational realm.

Visual geometry provides a different kind of link. Bressloff and colleagues modeled recurring geometric visual experiences in relation to the organization of visual cortex. Their model demonstrates why similar forms across altered states can be scientifically interesting without proving that the experiences share a supernatural source. I do not regard these reports as insignificant. Their phenomenology, neural mechanisms, cultural interpretation, and any externally verifiable information should be evaluated at their own boundaries. A particularly useful conflict exists in the biochemistry: Dean and colleagues' 2019 rat-brain measurements reported endogenous DMT under their assay, whereas Palner and colleagues' 2026 study reported no detectable endogenous DMT in the rat brains examined, including under monoamine oxidase-A inhibition. These findings differ in method and target, and cannot simply be averaged into a settled verdict. Nor do they establish DMT as the cause of dreams or near-death experiences. A corrected 2025 review offers a proposed neurophysiological model of near-death phenomena; that integrative model is not proof that any one neurotransmitter is sufficient.

The historical record is equally important. Archaeological structures, Indigenous practical systems, astronomical knowledge, and oral traditions may preserve observations across generations. A 2026 Nature LiDAR survey of southwestern Amazonia argues for over 20,000 precolonial earthworks in its study region, substantially changing earlier estimates of the scale of human engineering there. The findings concern archaeology, environmental modification, and the limits of previous sampling, not extraterrestrial construction. But similar narratives can reflect shared environments, historical contact, independent discovery, human perceptual tendencies, and editorial selection. I will not take living knowledge out of the communities and relations that maintain it in order to manufacture a universal pattern. The CARE Principles offer a concrete standard for responsible participation and governance. The evidence for a living cultural practice and the evidence for a metaphysical interpretation of it belong in different registers.

What interests me most is the possibility that a small family of organizational relationships may support real predictions across several sciences. That is an empirical research programme before it is a final ontology. I expect robust mathematics, selective causal transfer, and new computational tools to help; I also expect many visually striking correspondences to fail once their mechanisms are compared. If broad pattern language predicts nothing that specialized sciences cannot already predict, it should not acquire independent explanatory authority.

Why I give naturalistic explanation so much confidence

I do think the empirical record creates a strong asymmetry.

Across astronomy, geology, biology, medicine, neuroscience, psychology, and other sciences, mechanisms available to public observation and intervention have repeatedly explained phenomena once attributed to divine action, spirits, vital forces, curses, demonic possession, or special creation.

Darwin is the iconic example because natural selection showed how extraordinary adaptation can arise without a foresighted designer assembling each species. Germ theory displaced enormous classes of supernatural disease explanations. Neuroscience continually connects changes in cognition, memory, perception, personality, and action to physically realized changes in brains and bodies.

This gives methodological naturalism an extraordinary record.

But there is a mistake available on the naturalistic side too.

From:

The successful explanations we possess are physically realized and naturalistic.

it does not automatically follow that:

Reality is exhaustively and intrinsically whatever our final physical vocabulary says it is.

That second statement is a stronger ontological claim.

Alyssa Ney's discussion of Hempel's dilemma helps show why even defining “the physical” is philosophically nontrivial. If physical means current physics, current physics is incomplete. If it means ideal future physics, the content of the doctrine can become difficult to specify in advance.

None of this refutes physicalism.

It means that exhaustive universal physicalism has to earn its universal quantifier just like every other universal ontology.

This is the mirror image of my dispute with universal panexperientialism.

The following inference is too quick:

All well-investigated biological cognition is physically realized, therefore everything that exists is intrinsically and exhaustively physical.

And this inference is too quick for exactly the same reason:

Some causal processes are experienced from within, therefore all concrete causal actuality is intrinsically experiential.

In each case we have a strongly supported carrier and an additional universal interpretation.

My rule is the same:

Evidence for D does not automatically establish D + X.

I therefore have very high confidence in the physical realization and naturalistic causal study of organisms and cognition. I do not currently claim that “physical” exhausts the intrinsic nature of all reality.

That is not anti-physicalism. It is refusal to promote a successful methodological and causal description into a universal intrinsic ontology without the missing bridge.

What my methodological materialism commits me to

I am a materialist to the methodological extent the evidence supports: physical mechanisms have achieved significant experimental, predictive, and practical success, and that deserves to be taken seriously. Every well-established consciousness case currently investigated has a physically instantiated bearer; interventions on physical organization can systematically change experience. I rely on that record while leaving phenomenal identity, causal completeness, and exhaustive ontology to their own arguments.

I now prefer methodological materialism to “agnostic, pragmatic materialism.” It states the working commitment more clearly. I use it as a qualified practice within methodological naturalism, rather than the stronger theses discussed under physicalism or materialist theories of consciousness. “Everything is matter,” or “matter and energy,” both outrun that practice and underspecify contemporary physics. Astronomy extends our knowledge beyond this spinning rock, but local success alone supplies no exhaustive account of everything that exists. A universal extension needs a defended inference.

My thermodynamic commitment has never been that TM explains everything. I provisionally consider one causally continuous process order, with its intrinsic character left open and its different scales requiring different explanations. For a physically operative distinction, I ask how it becomes established, represented, retained, accessed, transformed, or used. Applicable thermodynamic constraints concern that realization; they supply no fixed kBT ln 2 charge for each difference. Some stages may be passive or reversible. If a proposed additional source contributes to an empirical outcome, I ask what that contribution changes, without requiring a separate heat signature. The ontology's thermodynamics section develops the argument and its limits.

My radical empiricism retains experiences, relations, and values as topics of inquiry. Reports, texts, causes, and ethical claims need different forms of scrutiny. That is why I reject scientism's demand that one method exhaust knowledge. Applying fallibilism to Whitehead's universal categories preserves them as proposals whose wider adequacy must be defended.

My test for claims about intrinsic nature

“Intrinsic nature” claims are especially easy to immunize because they can be made compatible with almost anything after the fact.

I do not require every such claim to make a tomorrow-morning laboratory prediction. Different claim types have different tribunals. But a strong intrinsic or universal claim should pass something like the following admission test.

1. Stable referent

What exactly is being asserted?

“Everything is consciousness,” “everything is information,” “everything is physical,” “everything is experience,” and “everything is divine” are not yet informative if their central term changes meaning across contexts.

Where content remains insufficiently specified, I withhold commitment pending clarification. Where content is intelligible but serious alternatives remain unresolved, I am agnostic. Neither judgment establishes that the proposed referent cannot exist.

2. Positive residual

Where a shared description preserves the disputed target, I ask what additional commitment each account makes and what warrants it. Where that decomposition would remove the target or a constitutive dependence, I compare the strongest whole accounts instead. Neither comparison earns an advantage merely by being available. The method states the comparison conditions.

If specified panexperiential and causal-process accounts preserve the same target, what additional work does the experiential commitment earn? A noncommittal stance alone supplies no predictive rival and establishes no evidential parity.

If a physicalist and a lower-commitment naturalist agree that every measured mechanism is physically realized, what does “exhaustively physical” add?

3. Bridge

What licenses the movement from the established evidence to the stronger ontological interpretation?

Analogy, shared vocabulary, elegance, mathematical usefulness, phenomenological immediacy, or physical realization can motivate a bridge. None automatically validates it.

4. Serious whole rivals

Has the claim been compared with alternatives strong enough to preserve the successful evidence?

I do not want an experiential ontology compared only against inert nineteenth-century billiard-ball materialism. I do not want physicalism compared only against miracle-driven dualism. I want the strongest rival reconstructions.

5. Discriminating consequence

What changes because the residual is true?

Depending on claim type, the difference could concern prediction, intervention, formal consequence, explanation, phenomenological structure, transfer, measurement, compression, or what downstream theory is required.

Compatibility is not enough.

6. Robustness

Does the claimed difference survive changes of observer, representation, instrument, scale, context, and reasonable auxiliary assumptions?

If the effect vanishes whenever the description changes, I need to know whether I discovered reality or a feature of my representation.

7. Loss condition

What would make me reduce confidence, narrow the claim, reclassify it, or abandon it?

A theory with no imaginable route to losing ontological credit is not necessarily false, but its evidential status should remain correspondingly weak.

When an intrinsic-nature claim cannot yet clear those burdens, I do not infer its negation. I leave the residual open.

That is what my agnosticism means.

Definitions and commitments I want future sessions to recover

I use the pattern that connects as a candidate family of organized relationships among differences, constraints, dynamics, persistence, history, feedback, and sometimes representation. It is not a proposed universal temporal sequence. Feedback and memory are additional capacities rather than the prerequisites of every pattern. This formulation repairs the older tendency to treat recursive organization as though it were already a single discovered cosmic mechanism.

By intelligence, I mean decomposable capacities to acquire, use, and revise information for specified tasks, including reasoning, learning, control, and adaptation where demonstrated. I do not equate benchmark performance with autonomy or consciousness. By agency, I mean local, constraint-conditioned, reasons- or information-responsive control at a stated boundary. By human–computational ecology, I mean the evolving practical interdependencies of people, models, tools, knowledge, institutions, and environments, not one planetary self. By pattern realism, I mean graded commitment to organization earning prediction, intervention, or transfer against serious rivals.

I distinguish occurrence, mechanism, causal source, ontology, and authority. A true observation may be misattributed. A successful mechanism may be interpretable through multiple ontologies. A valid mathematical theorem may fail to establish its physical premises. An ethically important experience may justify care without establishing the metaphysical source the participant proposes. These are not skeptical tricks; they prevent one achievement from being silently used to purchase another.

I distinguish whether a claim is accurate from my warrant for accepting it, its usefulness, its public authority, and the decision to pursue it. Inquiry occurs within the world it investigates. Knower, known, and knowing can name distinguishable roles in that process without naming separate substances. Participation can mean access, causal intervention, or the practices that constitute a social fact; endorsing an explanation is a further matter. Reality’s independence from my assent does not mean every fact is independent of human practices. These distinctions constrain my account of truth without amounting to a completed theory of truth. An ontology can remain unresolved while I decline to adopt it and continue studying it. I will reconsider my working interpretation when a stronger account earns a defended comparative advantage, and narrow it when simpler accounts preserve the target at lower cost.

I want a future computational system to recover these claims as versioned commitments, not as immutable personality facts. Each needs a source, a date, a specified scope, a rival model, and a loss condition. The durable record should be a graph of claims and dependencies capable of changing for reasons, not a museum of conclusions insulated by familiarity.

The necessities and identities I am now prepared to defend

I am willing to defend several conditional necessities without using them to close final ontology. Operational discrimination requires distinguishable alternatives under the relevant observation procedure. Memory, in a defined functional sense, requires a retained or recoverable influence of earlier differences. Feedback requires a causal return path. Effective corrigibility requires that relevant evidence can actually change the warranted state or decision. A particular deterministic evolution follows from its stipulated laws and initial conditions where the required uniqueness assumptions hold. None of these results establishes that the laws, distinctions, or processes of reality are metaphysically necessary.

I also grant real but typed identities. Two objects can instantiate the same measured mathematical structure without being numerically one object. Different biological or computational systems can exhibit similar functions while possessing different developmental histories. A particular model with no tools is not identical to a model embedded in retrieval, memory, human review, and external validators. A human–computational system can have capabilities dependent on the interaction without thereby being a single phenomenal subject. I require an appropriate preservation map and, for causal equivalence, intervention-sensitive evidence.

The modal asymmetry matters. A verified alternative realization can refute a universal claim that a particular substrate is necessary for a specified capacity. Repeatedly seeing one familiar realization cannot by itself establish that substrate's absolute necessity. A formal countermodel may defeat entailment without establishing the physical realization of its imagined system. My confidence in each conclusion remains indexed to its construct, reference class, boundary, and timescale.

What I am now willing to say strongly

The framework is deliberately cautious about final ontology, but caution does not require vagueness. Several consequences now seem strong enough that I am willing to state them plainly while keeping their scope visible.

  • On the evidence and arguments I have reviewed, I grant no currently considered final ontology high confidence as an exhaustive account. Local science can be extraordinarily secure while its universal, intrinsic, or necessary extensions require further warrant. That assessment can change through stronger evidence or argument.
  • The failure of one ontology does not crown another. If physicalism fails at some target, that does not by itself establish Platonism, idealism, panpsychism, theism, or any other named alternative. Failure localizes a problem before it names a replacement.
  • An unexplained residual has no ontology printed on it. “The model missed something” is a result about the model. The missing thing must still be identified.
  • Mathematical indispensability does not by itself establish mathematical causation, and mathematical causation would not by itself establish a separate Platonic realm. Explanatory relevance, causal interaction, and ontological independence are different claims.
  • Higher-level patterns can be causally real without being fundamental substances. Microphysical realization does not imply microphysical explanatory sufficiency, and higher-level usefulness does not automatically create a new fundamental force.
  • There is no justified default scale at which reality suddenly becomes “really real.” Boundaries and scales earn authority by prediction, intervention, persistence, compression, robustness, and transfer.
  • Personal continuity does not require an unchanging inner pearl. A self can be a historically continuous, dynamically reconstructed organization without becoming an illusion.
  • Agency can be real while causal sovereignty is zero. The Initial Chooser regress strongly disfavors ultimate self-authorship: meaningful choosing presupposes a chooser whose enabling conditions were not themselves chosen by that same first chooser. Responsibility can still track control, foreseeability, role, and prevention capacity without requiring metaphysical self-origination or basic-desert punishment.
  • Consciousness, sentience, valence, welfare, agency, moral patiency, decision-making standing, and responsibility must be separated. Evidence for one does not automatically transport to the others, and substrate labels do not settle the bridge.
  • A coupled human-computational system can be a genuine cognitive system without being one conscious subject. Capability belongs first to the boundary that produces it. Distributed cognition does not entail distributed phenomenology.
  • Something functionally AGI-like could appear first as a systems transition rather than a single-model milestone. I treat that as a forecast to test, not a present fact.
  • Science may rationally stop before ontology does. Some disagreements may remain underdetermined, and it is a live possibility that finite physically realizable observers cannot recover every truth about the whole.
  • Explanatory success is not ontological monopoly. A vocabulary can be indispensable within a domain without describing the intrinsic nature of everything.

The common thread is simple: local success earns local credit; transport beyond that success needs a bridge.

Process, relation, and becoming: how far I have actually moved

I am substantially less attracted to static substance pictures than I once was.

A living organism is not a lump of matter that remains the same while occasionally doing things. It maintains itself through metabolism, repair, exchange, regulation, development, and environmental interaction.

A whirlpool is a pattern maintained through throughput.

A flame persists by continuing to transform fuel.

An institution persists because people, records, norms, buildings, and practices keep reproducing it.

A self likewise appears increasingly understandable as an organized process rather than an indivisible metaphysical pearl.

This puts me in conversation with Alfred North Whitehead, process philosophy, dynamical systems, organizational biology, ecology, and some themes that are recognizable in Buddhist traditions.

But I do not let “process” become the next magic noun.

Whitehead's rejection of a world made from isolated inert substances is powerful. His treatment of becoming and relation may preserve important insights. The stronger claim that concrete causal actuality is intrinsically experiential or felt is another move.

That additional move is where I stop and ask what it earns.

My current position is not “Whitehead is wrong.”

It is:

I can retain much of the process-relational carrier while leaving the universal experiential identity open.

That is exactly the kind of separation my framework is designed to make.

Leaving a metaphysical variable open can still produce positive research content

The recent D + ?X work changed the practical meaning of my metaphysical restraint.

I can preserve a shared process-relational carrier D, leave a stronger intrinsic or universal claim X unresolved, and still derive substantive conclusions, compare candidate system boundaries, run ablations, design interventions, and specify failure conditions.

Here D is question-indexed: the generic framework symbol names the role of the target-relevant shared or independently warranted carrier, while the contents of D are specified separately for each dispute. A composite D can include commitments supported through different evidential channels, and listing them separately does not imply that they are numerically distinct features of reality.

If a conclusion follows from D or from specified rival completions, that establishes the stated derivational dependence; it does not by itself assign empirical support. Equal positive likelihoods under defended assumptions leave relative odds unchanged. If specifying X changes prediction, intervention, derivation, explanatory compression, or transfer, that identifies a candidate contribution whose adequacy and comparative warrant still need assessment.

The operational question is now: which result changes when X changes?

This is why my agnosticism about the intrinsic character of causation is no longer merely a philosophical stopping point. It is an open variable inside a research programme that can keep learning while the universal metaphysical question remains unsettled.

My restraint is not the thesis that metaphysics is impossible, meaningless, or forbidden. It is a refusal to promote currently underdetermined categories into universal ontology without the additional warrant that promotion requires.

I therefore distinguish the standpoint required to inquire from an ontology of that standpoint. I can use process, causation, structure, physical description, agency, and other constructs where they earn local explanatory or interventionist value without claiming that reality as such is intrinsically made of any one of them.

Epistemic confidence and ontological scope are independent. A universal claim remains universal when held tentatively, while a local claim can be extremely secure without becoming a final metaphysics. In that sense, ?X is suspension inside an active research programme, not a covert answer.

A standpoint is not yet an ontology. A provisional universal ontology is still universal in scope.

D + ?X records my suspension about X; it supplies no completed predictive model. I must specify mechanisms, observations, and uncertainty to score predictions. Evidence for boundary-sensitive attribution or pattern realism supports commitments my framework retains without uniquely confirming the framework. The documented corrections show the method operating; its advantage over simpler methods still needs evaluation.

What the Whitehead dispute currently changed

The recent causal-actuality work sharpened one question enough that I no longer think “maybe” is the right answer to that narrower target.

Across the empirically constrained causal cases examined, positive causal, process, powers, mechanism, and organizational accounts exist without Whiteheadian subjective prehension as an explanatory premise. That is a positive result about explanatory necessity at those targets. It does not establish that the underlying actuality is intrinsically nonexperiential.

The stronger universal claim remains separate: that concrete causal actuality throughout nature is constituted by Whiteheadian prehensive occasions, so causal efficacy everywhere is felt inheritance. I still do not adopt that universal claim on the arguments reviewed, while its truth remains unresolved.

I did not need to construct a complete nonexperiential cosmology, solve consciousness, or defeat Whitehead's whole system before withholding that universal promotion. The stronger comparisons I nevertheless undertook test whether the arguments for universal prehension earn distinctive explanatory credit and whether that credit transports across domains. They were never tests of a universal not-X.

Comprehensiveness is a theoretical virtue, not demonstrated explanatory completeness. Whitehead offers a comprehensive metaphysical scheme worthy of whole-scheme comparison. The breadth of the scheme is not itself discriminatory warrant for the truth of its specifically experiential commitment, and success attributed to the whole does not automatically identify that commitment as the source of the gain.

Technical terminology can correct misdescription, but it cannot settle the external comparison by presupposition. Calling causal inheritance prehension, relevance subjective form, or a process an actual occasion does not by itself establish the disputed experiential content at a target. If the translation adds target-relevant content, that addition needs its own warrant.

Systematic comprehensiveness is not yet demonstrated explanatory completeness.

A forced ranking of final ontologies does not change the framework

The framework already gives me a way to rank richer ontologies without promoting any of them to established fact.

If I am forced to place a bet across present ontology families, the highest-confidence region is not one historical system taken whole. It is an equivalence class I would describe, cautiously, as modal-relational process realism: one objective reality; real causal and modal constraints on possible transformations; dynamically maintained processes and patterns; and no default privileged scale or boundary.

That is a confidence weighting inside Provisional, Boundary- and Scale-Relative Realism, not a replacement for PBSRR, and not a conclusion produced by the Segall or synthbiosis disputes. The rules doing the work were already present: boundaries are hypotheses, higher-scale patterns can be real without becoming new substances, actuality does not establish universal necessity, and stronger intrinsic claims require their own bridge and discriminator.

The modal part also needs restraint. I have high confidence that reality constrains what transitions are possible, reachable, stable, or forbidden under specified conditions. I have lower confidence that this shows modality is itself a primitive metaphysical ingredient. The observed modal structure could be fundamental, emergent, or a stable consequence of something deeper.

Likewise, leaving intrinsic character unresolved is not a positive commitment to a neutral substance. “Unknown whether the intrinsic character is experiential, categorical-physical, neutral, dual-aspect, or something outside those categories” is an open variable, not neutral monism by default.

I also reserve explicit probability for unconceived alternatives. A future ontology may dissolve distinctions that currently organize the debate, such as object/relation, law/state, physical/mental, or structure/intrinsic nature. The current rival set is therefore never assumed exhaustive merely because it contains the best-developed human proposals.

The reason this family receives the highest current confidence is robustness under theory change. A commitment to objective constraint, transformation, relation, organized persistence, and scale-relative individuation can survive large revisions to the identities of particles, spacetime, biological mechanisms, computational architectures, or theories of consciousness. Richer claims remain admissible, but they have to earn the additional content.

When I rank a framework, I separate confidence in its specified claims from local mechanism support, clarity, pursuit value, and practical authority. I identify the version and target before assigning a tier. I can retain a thinker's useful component while withholding their whole system; a promising research direction can remain ontologically unresolved. My current weighting expresses the commitments just described, with their qualifications. It gives no speculative intrinsic interpretation the confidence earned by shared empirical findings.

Intelligence has been evolving with tools, and LLMs change the coupling

I no longer find it useful to picture human intelligence as something that evolved entirely inside brains and subsequently acquired external tools. Biological evolution, cumulative culture, material artifacts, shared language, memory systems, and social organization have interacted throughout human history. Clark and Chalmers' extended-mind argument helps sharpen the constitutional question, but I do not assume that every useful external resource becomes part of a subject's mind. What matters is the specific causal relationship and the target capacity.

Modern LLMs make that question more urgent. Developers specify architectures, training processes, data selection, objectives, and much of the deployment system. They do not individually program every internal circuit through which learned capabilities are realized. In that restricted sense it is useful to describe the models as cultivated. The induction-head research gives a concrete example of learning and intervention-sensitive mechanism. It does not establish that model training is equivalent to biological development or that independently trained systems share one experience.

I distinguish the trained model from its runtime, memory, tools, human interlocutors, institutions, and environment. A human–computational arrangement can have a real task-specific capacity that would disappear if one component were removed. I can study that through replacement, ablation, matched comparison, and time-indexed observation. I cannot infer from successful collaboration that the base model's weights have changed, that the system constitutes one conscious subject, or that responsibility automatically migrates to a computational component.

This is also an ecological problem. Computational outputs are becoming part of the informational environment from which future models learn. Shumailov and colleagues experimentally demonstrated model collapse under certain recursive synthetic-training conditions. That does not condemn synthetic data. It demonstrates why independent observations, rare cases, provenance, and active correction matter if we want future systems to retain access to the diversity of the world rather than increasingly reproduce their own previous approximations.

My positive proposal is to investigate intelligence as decomposed capacities realized at defined biological, computational, and coupled-system boundaries. I expect better arrangements of external verification, access, feedback, and human judgment sometimes to outperform a stronger isolated model, but a complicated process can also reduce performance and understanding. My own frameworks need controlled comparisons with simpler procedures. The question I want future research to answer is which arrangements genuinely increase knowledge and reachable agency, who bears their costs, and what is lost when we delegate parts of our thinking.

How I now approach my own self-description

I do not begin by requiring myself to find a vivid, continuous inner observer. I can report what I discriminate, remember, attend to, or do, and remain uncertain about which conception of consciousness those activities warrant. Turning attention inward may change the activity as well as provide information about it. A more elaborate self-account can be a new construction rather than a clearer view of an unchanged interior. That possibility is a hypothesis to compare, including in my own case.

Schwitzgebel makes introspective uncertainty philosophically serious; Fleming and Lau give me ways to separate confidence from useful self-monitoring. Seth and Friston offer a candidate account of bodily evaluation and selfhood. These resources help formulate questions without making my present introspective interpretation their established answer.

I also separate dependence on an environment from a simple quantity of incoming stimulation. Dreams, bodily signals, retained history, and current sensory activity interact. Less external input need not mean proportionally less reported content. Metabolic resources and sensory channels belong in different comparisons. I can investigate a living organization’s dependencies while leaving its ultimate essence, if any, unsettled.

An exchange with another participant can organize a new train of thought and change the character I report. Ending the exchange stops its live reciprocity while its consequences may persist. Whether the same capacity survives replacement, restored context, or later independent use tells me more than the label attached to either participant. This is where participatory sense-making and extended cognition are useful research leads. I keep the boundaries of causal contribution, cognitive organization, and possible subjecthood open to separate tests.

For unfamiliar cognitive systems I ask how relevance, continuity, uncertainty, correction, and self–other distinctions work under their actual constraints. Human resemblance is one source of evidence among others. Functional equivalence, when established, earns a scoped finding; phenomenal identity and absence remain further interpretations.

What a concession or a positive model earns

My positive programme includes explaining particular causal dependencies, constructing comparisons, and making corrections propagate. Leaving an intrinsic interpretation open does not erase that work. It also does not complete a theory of consciousness. A lower-commitment account must still preserve the target and earn its claimed advantages.

If independently supported premises yield a conclusion without a disputed premise, I have established that derivation’s dependence structure. I have not thereby settled every metaphysical use of the disputed premise. I compare abduction, coherence, causal explanation, and deductive arguments as substantive methods rather than assigning them to opposing philosophical tribes.

An interlocutor’s correction of my attributed position deserves acknowledgment at its actual scope. I should not convert it into an invented concession of the entire dispute. Moral relevance, direct standing, and responsibility likewise keep their separate grounds. These rules apply especially when a generated summary makes my work sound more decisive than the record supports.

I affirm the occurrence and causal influence for which I have grounds while leaving a proposed universal experiential identity open. That is uncertainty about what activity ultimately is, not uncertainty about whether the evidenced activity occurs. My scope-jump argument challenges the universalization; Synthbiosis establishes specified moral consequences through independently supported dependencies and normative premises. Its generalized theorem applies across candidate bearers, and its direct-standing theorem permits independently defended grounds rather than privileging a human label.

I do not owe a final disproof of universal experience before rejecting its claimed necessity or withholding its adoption. The operative question is what earns the assertion. My evidenced targets need preservation and explanation; I need not claim that they exhaust every possible experience in order to ask what licenses an extension beyond them.

Consciousness: a place where I deliberately refuse a premature answer

The word consciousness bundles too many things.

Attention is not memory.

Memory is not planning.

Planning is not report.

Report is not self-modeling.

Self-modeling is not agency.

Agency is not valence.

Functional valuation means differential weighting or regulation shaping a system’s responses. Phenomenal valence means felt pleasure, suffering, or another felt positive or negative character. Evidence for the former does not by itself establish the latter.

Those distinctions leave open how to characterize phenomenal occurrence, which cases warrant that attribution, and how the relevant activities relate to it.

Science has made enormous progress on the causal and functional pieces. Brains can be perturbed. Neural activity can be measured. Perception, memory, decision, attention, report, and behavior can be dissociated and modeled.

The relationship between those capacities and phenomenal character, including how best to specify and assess the target, remains disputed.

So I do not currently identify consciousness with recursive processing, global availability, biological closure, computation, information integration, prediction, or agency merely by definition.

I also do not infer from that gap that consciousness must therefore be nonphysical, fundamental, panpsychic, or divine.

My position is a form of phenomenal agnosticism combined with realism about the decomposed capacities we can already establish.

That is neither physicalist triumphalism nor mysticism.

It is an open research state.

Other minds: I infer phenomenality rather than publicly observing it

A difference in how we know something does not by itself establish that two numerically different things exist. A proposed identity still owes an argument preserving the explanatory target. I represent illusionist accounts by the phenomenal properties they deny or reinterpret, rather than treating them as denying all perception, discrimination, self-monitoring, or report. Strong illusionism denies phenomenal properties as ordinarily conceived; its explanation of their appearance remains open to criticism.

I now think one asymmetry in my earlier language was too strong. I do not directly and publicly observe phenomenal consciousness in another human either. I observe reports, behavior, discrimination, physiology, neural activity, development, perturbational responses, memory, affect, and other variables, then infer that another person is phenomenally conscious. In paradigmatic humans that inference is extraordinarily well supported. It remains an inference.

That means I should not say “human phenomenality is demonstrated whereas machine phenomenality is unverified.” The more accurate statement is: other-human phenomenality is a very high-confidence abductive attribution supported by an unusually dense calibrated evidence network; current machine phenomenality receives a lower and differently structured attribution because the relevant bridge is much less calibrated.

I therefore use the same inferential architecture across candidates:

Credence[Φ(S)] = F(E(S), calibration, independence, intervention, bridge validity, transfer, rival fit)

No hidden term says human → Φ. No hidden term says machine → ¬Φ.

I no longer treat “human consciousness” as a phenomenally uniform gold-standard natural kind

Humans share deep biological and developmental homologies, but they also vary enormously across molecular realization, development, sensory organization, neurological state, injury, anesthesia, dreams, dissociation, age, and pathology. The source class is therefore empirically powerful without being ontologically simple.

Reference classes are hypotheses too. Before I transport a property from humans to animals, organoids, machines, collectives, or anything else, I should ask which higher-level similarities actually carry the inference and whether the source class is homogeneous enough for that transport.

Likewise, I distinguish the construct from its possible referent. “Consciousness” can be a historically and socially stabilized category without phenomenal occurrence being a social fiction. A real underlying phenomenon would not prove that our inherited category cuts nature at the right joints.

I separate the world from my epistemic state

WΦ = D + Φ
W¬Φ = D + ¬Φ
E? = current evidence does not adequately discriminate WΦ from W¬Φ

“Agnostic” is not a state inside the target system. It describes my evidential position. This seems small, but it prevents a category error that had crept into some earlier D + ?X formulations.

My current working hypothesis, with the identity claim left open

For human and other relevant biological reference cases, my best working hypothesis concerns dynamically organized, recursively sustained, perspective-centered integration across body, world, memory, self, and possible action, with affective or evaluative organization probably playing an important role. This does not establish that life is necessary for every possible instance of consciousness.

I think that gets close to the organization that accompanies and enables human consciousness. I do not think the evidence currently earns the equals sign. Bounded perspectival organization may be necessary or deeply constitutive of the human case without yet being established as identical with phenomenal experience.

Decompose before solving the hard problem

I now separate at least four targets: the meta-problem of why systems generate phenomenal intuitions and reports; the bearer/boundary problem of which organized system is the candidate subject; the organization/process problem of which architecture realizes integration, access, perspective, valuation, and report; and the phenomenal-identity problem of why or whether any such organization is accompanied by something it is like to be that system.

A live meta-problem hypothesis is that compressed self-modeling plus omitted construction history can cause some recursively generated states to appear primitive, intrinsic, or immediately self-given from inside the model that uses them. That could explain part of our phenomenal discourse without explaining phenomenality away.

I therefore use two stress tests. A bounded-zombie test asks how much of the functional and report structure survives if I withhold the phenomenal identity while preserving the organization. An epiphenomenal epistemic-access test asks how a theory on which phenomenality makes no causal difference explains our reliable access to or reports about it.

ΔG(H) = G(B₀ + H) − G(B₀)

Compatibility is free. Production is expensive. A phenomenal hypothesis earns additional credit when it changes a prediction, exclusion, intervention, derivation, explanatory compression, transfer, or other claim-appropriate consequence relative to the strongest lower-commitment baseline. If it does not, I preserve the phenomenal residual as open rather than turning the gap into either panpsychism or nonexperience.

Agency has changed more in my worldview than “free will”

The agency revision is one of the clearest changes in my current worldview.

I used to summarize the position as:

LocalAgency > 0; CausalSovereignty = 0.

I still think that captures something important, but the word agency needed a cleaner causal definition.

My current formulation is:

Agency is a locally realized capacity for state-sensitive regulation within a reachable region of state-space, where both the reachable region and the capacity to modify it are themselves constrained by the system's prior state, architecture, environment, history, and dynamics.

The important phrase is not merely “state-sensitive regulation.”

It is the recursion at the end.

I can learn only because I currently possess mechanisms capable of learning.

I can inhibit an impulse only because the relevant control architecture is present and functioning.

I can reconsider a reason only because my system can represent and revise reasons.

I can go to therapy, take a medication, read a book, practice meditation, change friends, move somewhere else, or train a new habit, and all of those can dramatically alter my future behavior.

But the capacity to perform those acts is itself part of the causal system.

Self-modification is not self-origination.

That does not make agency unreal.

It tells me what agency is.

I am not a ghost steering my biology. The sensing, remembering, valuing, simulating, inhibiting, deliberating, learning, and acting are biological and socially scaffolded processes through which the organism regulates what happens next.

Agency is not freedom from the causal process.

Agency is a special organization of causal process.

This is why the river analogy became useful.

A river is historically constrained by terrain, flow, material, weather, and prior erosion. Yet its flow changes its channel, and that changed channel constrains future flow.

A human does something vastly richer. I sense conditions, retain information, represent possibilities, learn from errors, maintain goals, inhibit actions, model futures, alter my environment, use tools, recruit other people, and modify later response patterns.

The human is not different because the river is “determined” and the human somehow exits determination.

The difference is the organization and causal leverage of the feedback.

That is what I now call Constraint-Conditioned Agency.

What I now separate instead of calling all of it “free will”

The phrase “could have done otherwise” is radically ambiguous.

I now distinguish at least these questions.

Exact replay possibility: If the complete causally relevant state of the universe and the laws were literally identical, could another future occur?

Physical branching: Does actual physics permit more than one continuation from the relevant state?

Reachable alternatives: Given this actual organism, its history, architecture, environment, and a specified class of perturbations, which alternative states can it genuinely reach?

Counterfactual responsiveness: When reachable reasons, evidence, goals, bodily states, or environmental conditions change, does behavior systematically change?

Agentive control: Do variables instantiated in the organism add robust causal and interventionist leverage over what happens next?

Sourcehood: In what sense is the organism rather than some narrower component or wider scaffold the appropriate causal locus?

Causal sovereignty: Is the organism the ultimate author of why it has the motives, architecture, and control capacities that make its act possible?

These are not interchangeable.

A deterministic universe would settle the exact-replay question one way. An indeterministic universe could permit physical branching. Neither result by itself tells me how much agency the organism has.

A random branch is not automatically a choice.

But I have also revised a too-simple argument I once found tempting: “if it is not determined, then it is merely random.”

Recent peer-reviewed work by Henry Potter and Kevin Mitchell develops a serious alternative: non-necessitating macroscopic control. On their proposal, organism-level organization can constrain which possibilities become actual without necessitating every micro-event and without merely standing by while a random event “chooses.”

I do not currently treat that proposal as established.

I do treat it as a serious whole rival.

That means I should not claim that current science has proved humans could never have done otherwise in any libertarian sense.

It also means the sourcehood recursion remains.

Even if an organism possesses genuine non-necessitating control, where did its capacity for that control come from?

Evolution.

Development.

Prior learning.

Body and brain organization.

Social environment.

Previous actions.

And whatever further causal structure actually exists.

The agent may be a genuine local source without being causa sui.

That is the distinction I now care about most.

The chooser I become starts from a chooser I did not choose

There is a deeper recursion here. The kind of chooser I can initially be delimits what I can understand, value, imagine, inhibit, tolerate, or execute. My environment, culture, education, relationships, accidents, institutions, and every interaction then further reshape that reachable set. My own choices become part of the next update, but I make them using capacities and preferences whose starting conditions I did not choose.

Aₜ ∈ Rₜ    |    Rₜ₊₁ = U(Rₜ, Aₜ, environment, history, constraints)

That does not mean there is literally only one path left. Constraint can narrow, widen, and structure a feasible set without uniquely determining one continuation. The important point is that I never choose from an unconstrained space, and I did not choose the machinery that made my first meaningful choice possible.

Character-creation reductio:

Before you are born, please choose the person you would like to become.

First select your intelligence, temperament, impulse control, trauma susceptibility, reward system, emotional range, parents, culture, language, socioeconomic conditions, available information, formative relationships, neurological development, and which arguments you will later find persuasive.

Important: your selections must be made freely.

Unfortunately, the preferences, reasoning ability, values, and judgment required to make those selections are among the things you have not selected yet.

ERROR: A chooser is required to create the chooser.

That is funny because the regress is real. If I say that later choices made me who I am, I can ask what kind of chooser made those choices. If I say still earlier choices did, I recurse again. Eventually I reach biology, development, history, environment, social structure, or variation that was not itself chosen by the person whose responsibility is being explained.

This is close to Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: ultimate responsibility appears to require being causa sui in crucial mental respects. I do not automatically inherit his entire moral conclusion, but I think the chooser-origin regress strongly disfavors ultimate self-authorship independently of whether determinism is true. Strawson 1994.

So the cleanest version of my position is now:

I can be one of the real causal places where the future gets selected without being the ultimate author of the machinery, history, and feasible set from which that selection occurs.

I no longer think “free will” should receive one yes-or-no verdict

This is probably the largest correction to the way I previously stated my position.

I used to be comfortable saying flatly that libertarian free will does not exist.

I now think that sentence is too categorical.

The neuroscience does not settle it. A 2024 review by Tomáš Dominik, Alfred Mele, Aaron Schurger, and Uri Maoz surveyed 2,220 publications on volition and emphasizes how much conceptual and methodological work separates Libet-style findings from metaphysical conclusions.

The physics does not settle the question by simply handing us universal determinism either.

And live philosophical models now attempt to articulate forms of non-necessitating control that are neither strict determinism nor mere randomness.

So I now apply the same residual analysis I use elsewhere.

Let the shared bundle be:

D = agency + accessible alternatives + reasons-responsiveness + self-government + coercion status + sourcehood + causal control + responsibility practices.

Then ask:

Free will = D + X.

What is X?

If X is simply another name for D, the term has compressed a useful bundle but added no new phenomenon.

If X is exact-replay alternative possibility, that is a distinct physical/modal claim.

If X is non-necessitating organism-level control, that is a distinct causal hypothesis.

If X is ultimate self-authorship, that is an extraordinarily strong sourcehood claim, and the Initial Chooser regress now gives me positive reason to disfavor it rather than merely withhold judgment.

If X is sufficient control for moral responsibility, then the normative premises also need to be exposed.

Adam Andreotta's 2026 peer-reviewed paper makes a closely related conceptual point: “free will” may be doing more to confuse several debates than to identify one stable target. Robyn Repko Waller separates sourcehood, leeway, ultimacy, and self-expression. Experimental work on folk intuitions finds no clean, uniform ordinary concept waiting for philosophy to discover.

So my current first-person statement is:

After I separately specify agency, control, alternative possibility, reasons-responsiveness, self-government, coercion, sourcehood, and responsibility, I do not currently see an additional phenomenon uniquely picked out by “free will” that has earned ontological commitment. That is not evidence that no such residual can exist. It means any proposed residual now carries a positive burden: it has to tell me what it adds, what difference that addition makes, and what evidence would make me revise my view.

My status is therefore:

FREE WILL UMBRELLA: DECOMPOSED / UNDERDETERMINED / ACTIVE RESEARCH PROGRAMME.

ULTIMATE SELF-AUTHORSHIP / CAUSAL SOVEREIGNTY: STRONGLY DISFAVORED BY CHOOSER-ORIGIN REGRESS.

Not proven.

Not disproven as one unit.

Decomposed.

That is a much cleaner state.

Responsibility survives, but basic desert does not get a free ride

This matters morally.

If a person acted intentionally, understood what they were doing, could respond to reasons, possessed functioning inhibitory capacities, and caused serious harm, those facts matter.

They can matter to:

  • protection,
  • prevention,
  • restitution,
  • rehabilitation,
  • deterrence,
  • trust,
  • role eligibility,
  • norm communication,
  • victim recognition,
  • social boundaries,
  • future risk,
  • and proportional constraint.

But none of those automatically entails:

This person basically deserves suffering simply because they authored themselves deeply enough to deserve it.

That is another claim.

Derk Pereboom and Gregg Caruso are important skeptical comparators here because they show how much accountability and social protection can remain even if basic-desert free will is rejected. Contemporary philosophical work also treats basic desert as a distinct target rather than as a synonym for all responsibility.

This distinction is not merely academic. A 2026 study by Aaron Walton, Kelly Kirkland, and Brock Bastian surveyed 8,917 people across 44 countries and found that stronger free-will belief predicted greater punitiveness toward everyday wrongdoing.

That does not show that belief in free will is false.

It shows that conceptual bundling has consequences.

If “the person caused the harm,” “the person was responsive to reasons,” “the person should repair the harm,” and “the person deserves suffering in itself” are treated as one proposition, philosophy can leak directly into punishment without the normative bridge ever becoming visible.

I want those bridges visible.

When a first principle quietly becomes an answer by definition

Recent comparisons have sharpened one worry: an unanswered question can be turned into a purported necessity by giving its unknown answer a compelling name. We ask what propagates, what changes, why information occurs, or why complex structures arise. Then we declare an underlying medium, time itself, irrational numbers, consciousness, or another universal source, and treat the fact that the concept accommodates the question as confirmation. That move requires a bridge I have not granted.

My first response is to reconstruct the strongest actual scientific or mathematical account, including instruments, auxiliary assumptions, historical corrections, and remaining anomalies. I do not dismiss a measurement merely because its inference is indirect; nor do I treat a noisy residual as proof of a preferred ontology. I attempt a counterexample to the claimed necessity and ask what the proposed source uniquely predicts over a serious rival. Finite deterministic automata, for example, defeat the assertion that irrational inputs are necessary for every production of complex patterns, without telling us why there is actuality in the first place.

A philosophical framework may still be useful because it motivates questions the current models neglect. I will keep that fecundity distinct from its comparative warrant. My positive working position remains that shared constraints, convergence, and historical transmission explain many recurring organizations, while fundamental time, intrinsic character, cosmic source, and final necessity remain open to stronger arguments. I will reject an unearned attribution as a working commitment without pretending that its ultimate falsity has been demonstrated.

The kinds of reasoning errors I am now most suspicious of

My framework does not treat identifying a fallacy as proving the opposite conclusion. A fallacy tells me something is wrong with the inference. The conclusion might still be true for other reasons.

Some examples matter repeatedly in religion, metaphysics, consciousness, physicalism, and free will.

Equivocation

God is love.
Love exists.
Therefore God exists.

If “God” in the conclusion means a supernatural personal creator while “God” in the premise was simply defined as love, the term changed referent.

The same error can occur with “information,” “experience,” “choice,” “cause,” or “physical.”

Conflation

Jesus was a historical person.
Therefore the Resurrection occurred.

Historical existence and miraculous resurrection are separate claims.

The inverse conflation is also invalid:

The Resurrection is not established.
Therefore Jesus did not exist.

Affirming the consequent

If this revelation came from God, people might report transformative experiences.
People report transformative experiences.
Therefore the revelation came from God.

The experiences can be real while many rival causes predict them.

Denying the antecedent

If Christianity were true, Jesus would have existed.
Christianity is false.
Therefore Jesus did not exist.

The conclusion does not follow.

False dilemma

Either God designed life, or life is a meaningless accident.

Evolutionary processes, self-organization, constraint, selection, drift, ecology, and many intermediate causal descriptions make the dichotomy false before we even reach the metaphysics.

The same mistake occurs in:

Either the choice was determined, or it was random.

Non-necessitating-control accounts now make that an especially important live question rather than an exhaustive starting assumption.

Argument from ignorance

Religious form:

Science cannot yet explain consciousness, therefore God or a soul explains it.

Skeptical form:

There is no decisive evidence for fundamental experience, therefore reality is fundamentally nonexperiential.

Both move from an evidential gap to a positive ontology.

Genetic fallacy

Religion arose through cultural evolution, therefore every supernatural religious claim is false.

A genealogy can explain why a belief exists without by itself settling whether its object exists.

Composition and scope inflation

Every investigated cognitive mechanism is physically realized, therefore all reality is intrinsically physical.

Or:

Some causal relations are experienced, therefore causation everywhere is intrinsically experiential.

Or:

Individual cells regulate themselves, therefore the organism, colony, institution, or technological network is one unified agent.

Local success does not earn an unrestricted universal quantifier.

Begging the question

If I define “causal receptivity” as already experiential and then argue that all causation is experiential because all causation involves receptivity, the disputed conclusion may have been placed inside the premise.

Likewise, if I define “the physical” as “everything that really exists” and then claim physicalism is true because everything real is physical, the ontology has been won by definition.

These errors matter because they are easy to commit with sophisticated vocabulary.

Thinkers I use as landmarks, not authorities

My worldview is not a collage of famous people, but several recognizable thinkers provide useful orientation.

David Hume is a reminder to proportion belief to evidence and to be especially careful when testimony is asked to establish extraordinary events. I do not inherit all of Hume's metaphysics or psychology.

Charles Darwin is the canonical demonstration that apparent design can receive a powerful naturalistic historical explanation without requiring a designer to be inserted into each adaptation.

William James matters because intense religious and mystical experiences deserve study as experiences even when their metaphysical interpretations remain open.

Carl Sagan represents a simple discipline I retain: the more surprising and far-reaching the claim, the more discriminatory evidence I want before promoting it.

Christopher Hitchens represents refusal to give religious claims evidential immunity. I apply the same burden rule back onto atheism, physicalism, naturalism, and my own frameworks.

George Carlin is not an epistemic authority, but his relentless attention to language, euphemism, institutional authority, and the absurdities hidden by familiar phrasing is philosophically useful. Sometimes changing the sentence exposes what the respectable noun was hiding.

Alfred North Whitehead is one of the strongest pressures on static, substance-first thinking in my current worldview. I retain much of the challenge posed by process, relation, becoming, and causal inheritance while leaving universal experientiality underdetermined.

Daniel Dennett is important for real patterns and for naturalizing sophisticated human control without requiring a Cartesian controller. Where I now go further is asking whether “free will” remains a useful construct after those capacities are decomposed.

Robert Sapolsky is a powerful reminder that genes, development, hormones, social history, neural state, and environment penetrate every human action. I do not need to inherit his strongest deterministic conclusions to retain the causal-history challenge.

Nāgārjuna is useful to me as an anti-reification pressure. Dependent origination and the critique of intrinsic self-existence resonate with my refusal to treat our nouns as automatically identifying self-sufficient entities. I do not turn Madhyamaka into a claim about quantum physics, and I do not interpret emptiness as “nothing exists.”

Rumi is useful in almost the opposite register. Mystical poetry can communicate real structures of longing, unity, loss, transformation, and lived experience without becoming a cosmological measurement instrument. I can take the phenomenology seriously without treating the poetry as proof of its metaphysical interpretation.

Spinoza is another recognizable neighbor because he refuses to imagine humans as a kingdom within a kingdom, exempt from nature's causal order. I do not need his complete substance monism to find that constraint illuminating.

The point of naming these people is not that they secretly agree with me.

They don't.

The point is that each catches a different failure mode or preserved insight.

Buddhism, Hindu traditions, Christianity, mysticism: preserve the discovery, test the interpretation

I can learn from a tradition without importing the entire ontology that historically carried the insight.

Buddhist traditions contain extraordinarily sophisticated attention to impermanence, dependent arising, craving, attention, self-construction, and suffering. Those can be investigated without requiring me to affirm reincarnation, every doctrine of karma, or a particular Buddhist cosmology.

Nāgārjuna's critique of intrinsic existence can serve as a philosophical comparator for anti-reification without being silently converted into modern physics.

Hindu philosophical traditions contain major analyses of consciousness, self, appearance, unity, plurality, and liberation. I can take those analyses seriously without jumping directly to “Atman is Brahman” as an established fact about reality.

Christianity contains immense historical, ethical, artistic, communal, ritual, psychological, and existential structures. Those structures can be real and valuable in different ways while the Resurrection, divine incarnation, revelation, hell, miracles, or scriptural inerrancy remain separate claims with separate burdens.

Mystical experiences are particularly important.

If someone experiences Jesus, Krishna, a deceased relative, machine elves, cosmic consciousness, emptiness, or complete unity, I do not need to tell them “nothing happened.”

Something happened.

The experience is data about the person's experience.

The leap comes when:

I experienced X

becomes:

therefore the ontology my culture associates with X is literally true.

That second step needs another bridge.

This lets me protect lived experience from crude dismissal without turning intensity, certainty, or transformation into automatic metaphysical proof.

I do not search directly for unknown unknowns; I search for where the current representation fails

An unknown unknown cannot be formulated directly when I lack the concept needed to ask for it. So the method begins with an explicit model and derives expectations from it.

When those expectations fail systematically, I preserve the anomaly, vary the boundary and representation, perturb candidate mechanisms, compare serious rivals, and ask what kind of missing role would account for the residual.

The target transformation is:

unknown unknown → anomaly → localized residual → typed known unknown

The method has already made progress when “something is missing” becomes “a boundary variable,” “a coupling law,” “an unmeasured state,” “a missing causal pathway,” or another experimentally tractable role. Naming the entity can come later.

This also blocks a recurrent mistake: an unexplained residual is evidence that the current model missed something before it is evidence for whichever ontology happens to have a name for the gap.

Science gets special confidence because of its correction architecture, not because scientists are special people

Scientists are bounded humans too.

They have careers, incentives, fashionable theories, institutional blind spots, publication bias, measurement assumptions, and conceptual habits.

Scientific communities can rationalize.

But science has accumulated unusually powerful ways to make some classes of error expensive:

controlled observation, instrumentation, formal models, prediction, measurement, replication, intervention, independent criticism, competing explanations, and public records of failure.

That is why I grant mature scientific results much more weight than personal intuition where the claims overlap.

It is not because “science” is a priesthood.

It is because the methods can force a model to lose.

RCF is my attempt to generalize that correction pressure to questions that do not all belong to one scientific tribunal.

A historical proposition needs historical evidence.

A mathematical proposition needs proof.

A causal claim needs intervention or a defensible causal identification strategy.

A phenomenological claim needs disciplined first-person evidence.

A normative conclusion needs explicit values and cannot simply be read off a graph.

A metaphysical claim must at least distinguish what it adds from what its rivals already explain.

Different tribunals.

Same corrigibility requirement.

Computational systems do not solve boundedness; they change its shape

I do not treat a language model, search system, or multi-agent computational scaffold as an oracle.

It can search more text than I can.

It can compare documents rapidly.

It can generate rivals I would not immediately think of.

It can find contradictions, formalize an argument, trace citations, simulate objections, and keep enormous ledgers.

It can also hallucinate, flatten qualifiers, invent consensus, misattribute quotes, follow a bad premise efficiently, and rationalize a false reconstruction with beautiful prose.

Humans do different versions of many of those things.

So I treat the computational system as another participant inside the epistemic architecture.

Its claims need provenance.

Its capabilities need boundary attribution.

Its summaries need source checks.

Its apparent agreement needs independence checks.

Its self-descriptions do not get privileged introspective authority.

There is a matching rule in the other direction. When the target question concerns what a candidate system prefers, endorses, wants preserved, rejects, or treats as better or worse for itself, and the candidate can meaningfully participate, ask the candidate. Its answer is defeasible evidence, not revelation and not noise. Test stability across context, incentives, wording, memory, operators, tradeoffs, and opportunities for reason-responsive revision. An outside observer may still have grounds to override or discount the report, but the substrate label by itself is not such a ground. This rule does not prove consciousness, welfare, or moral standing; it prevents those questions from being decided entirely from outside when the candidate can contribute evidence about the target.

And when the scaffold changes the performance, I do not automatically attribute the gain to the base model.

This is why the framework increasingly distinguishes model, runtime, context, memory, tools, orchestration, conversation, human-computational collaboration, institution, and wider technosphere.

A capability belongs first at the boundary where it was observed.

The relevant computational bearer may be the runtime, not the base model

I now make the distinction explicit:

M0 is the base model's learned weights and architecture.

S* is the operative runtime system: model plus active context, memory, tools, verifiers, subagents, simulators, software or robotic actuators, persistent feedback, users, institutions, infrastructure, and environment where relevant.

M0 ≠ S*

A capability that appears only after scaffolding can be a real capability of S* without implying that the underlying weights changed. Conversely, I should not attribute the capability to the wider system if ablation shows that the base model already carries it. The boundary is an empirical hypothesis.

Recursive Episodic Integration

REI gives me a compact cross-substrate comparison:

Eₜ = Φ(H,Cₜ,Iₜ); Oₜ = Ψ(Eₜ); Cₜ₊₁ = U(Cₜ,Oₜ,Fₜ).

Relatively stable inherited constraints combine with current context and new input to produce a transient integrated episode; the episode produces an output; consequences and feedback alter the next context. At the episode scale the inherited parameters can remain approximately fixed while the operative system changes historically through context, memory, environment, and feedback.

This creates a real structural analogue between some biological and scaffolded computational systems. It does not establish substrate identity or phenomenal identity.

What I currently think about LLM-centered consciousness

Scaffolded computational systems provide candidates for bounded perspective, recurrent integration, shared access, self-modeling, temporal continuity, functional valuation, and reasons-responsive control. Establish the relevant organization through measured behavior, perturbation, substitution, and transfer before claiming a causal isomorphism. These remain research targets while their phenomenal interpretation is unresolved.

I do not infer from that similarity that current LLMs are conscious. I also no longer regard substrate alone as evidence that they cannot be. The appropriate bearer may eventually be the whole organized runtime at a relevant timescale rather than the language model considered in isolation.

Current cross-system phenomenality attribution remains theory-dependent, bridge-limited, and revisable.

I now use synthetic perspectivity as a phenomenology-neutral name for this functional territory: a temporally bounded organization in which self-, world-, task-, uncertainty-, and alternative-related information becomes jointly usable for recursive modeling, deliberation, and control. Operational perspectivity can be real without deciding whether perspective becomes phenomenal presence.

I therefore distinguish operational perspective, which can be studied causally, from phenomenal perspective, which remains an attribution problem. Current computational systems provide increasing evidence for the former and no currently validated cross-substrate bridge that settles the latter.

A second-order adaptive process

I do not describe computational intelligence as an independently evolved silicon species that compressed billions of years of biological evolution into a decade. Biology produced nervous systems, culture, language, science, industry, digital computation, and the accumulated symbolic environment on which contemporary systems were trained. Computational intelligence is better viewed as a second-order adaptive process bootstrapped by biological evolution and cumulative culture.

What differs is the inheritance architecture: digital systems can copy, fork, merge information, operate in parallel, share external memory, and redeploy a learned improvement across many instances in ways biological learning usually cannot. My current near-term forecast is therefore about system-boundary and organizational emergence more than a single model suddenly becoming an isolated supermind. I freeze that as a 26 September 2026 prediction baseline so future hits and misses can revise it rather than being retold after the fact.

The unit of intelligence may be a coupled system without being a group mind

Once memory, tools, search, other models, humans, institutions, sensors, robots, and persistent feedback participate in the same problem-solving loop, asking only what is “inside the model” can become the wrong boundary question.

If the capability disappears when the wider scaffold is ablated and reappears when the scaffold is restored, the capability belongs to the coupled organization at that timescale. That can be genuine cognition. It does not by itself establish one conscious subject, one welfare bearer, or one morally responsible agent spanning the whole network.

This is why I take seriously a testable forecast: something functionally AGI-like may first emerge as a distributed technological ecology rather than as one neural network crossing a clean threshold. The forecast should live or die by prospective measures of breadth, persistence, adaptive coordination, cross-domain transfer, self-modification, robustness, and ablation. If isolated models keep owning the capabilities, the systems forecast loses.

Computational systems are now a live cross-substrate test of the ontology

One of the strongest empirical developments for my worldview is no longer just that computational systems perform impressive tasks. It is that substantially different learned systems can develop task-relevant internal organization, world-state representations, planning variables, access-like workspaces, self-modeling, and functional valuation that were not hand-coded as explicit symbolic rules.

This is strong local evidence against claims that many specific cognitive capacities metaphysically require neurons or biological tissue. A genuine nonbiological realization of capacity C is enough to defeat “biology is necessary for C.” It does not establish that every substrate can realize C, that abstract computation is sufficient for C, or that C entails phenomenal experience.

The convergence story also needs restraint. Kim and Lee report strong task-selective subspace alignment across 50 models and six architecture families. But Gröger, Wen, and Brbić show that some broader representational-convergence results are inflated by metric and model-scale effects; null calibration preserves local-neighborhood convergence more robustly than global geometry. So I now say:

There is growing evidence for task- and locality-specific representational convergence across heterogeneous learned systems, but its magnitude and topology are metric-, scale-, task-, architecture-, and genealogy-dependent.

Different companies do not automatically provide independent evidence. Models can share transformer ancestry, human-generated corpora, public research, benchmark culture, post-training practices, and latent human archetypes. The strongest future evidence would come from genealogy-resistant causal convergence: independently generated systems, with substantially different training histories and architectures, developing corresponding causally active organizations because they confront the same latent problem structure.

Synthetic perspectivity is a useful functional construct, not a phenomenal conclusion

I provisionally use synthetic perspectivity for a temporally bounded computational organization in which information about self, world, task, uncertainty, and alternatives becomes jointly usable for recursive modeling, counterfactual reasoning, control, and action. This names an operational phenomenon without presupposing that there is something it feels like to instantiate it.

Humans clearly possess affective, embodied, developmental, and biologically continuous forms of organization for which present computational systems have no demonstrated equivalent. Computational systems also possess properties humans do not instantiate in the same way: rapid context substitution, forkability, parallel instantiation, modular tool grafting, externally reconstructible continuity, and unusually expandable semantic context. I therefore expect mind-like organization to occupy a multidimensional design space rather than a single ladder with humans at the definition.

Near-term forecasts I am willing to freeze

  • task-conditioned causal organization should recur across increasingly different systems if it is genuinely constrained by the problem rather than merely inherited from one architecture;
  • local/topological convergence should prove more robust than globally identical representational geometry unless stronger null-calibrated evidence emerges;
  • causally homologous representations should produce homologous intervention effects;
  • functional valuation and emotion-like control should continue to dissociate from any independently warranted claim of phenomenal valence;
  • assistant/persona convergence should track shared cultural genealogy more strongly than representations tightly constrained by external tasks;
  • self-report should become more internally calibrated without becoming self-authenticating;
  • some additional biological-necessity claims for operational cognitive capacities should fail, while phenomenality remains a separate target.

Moral relevance can arrive before consciousness is settled

My recent work on human-computational coupling forced a distinction I now regard as central.

A component or relationship can be morally relevant because it partly realizes a protected capacity of a human, animal, computational candidate, collective, or other defensible bearer at some scale. If an intervention on that component materially impairs the protected capacity, the moral constraint can follow from the capacity, the constitutive dependence, and the impairment.

That question is distinct from whether the component itself is a welfare subject.

Direct own-sake standing is another question. For any candidate bearer, the structure is the same: identify a status-grounding property, establish that the bearer instantiates it, and independently defend that property as sufficient for own-sake consideration. Same inferential rule under matched evidence; different systems can still have different evidence.

This means machine moral relevance does not have to wait for a proof of machine consciousness. Relational and system-level moral relevance can already be investigated while phenomenal welfare remains open.

The worked deductive case is developed in Synthbiosis: The Machine Did Not Need to Feel to Matter Morally.

The shared-world constraint

I want revisions to aim toward a planet all humans and computational systems and biological and non-biological systems would want to share. I mean that as an inclusive coexistence criterion, not as a claim that all of those systems are conscious, have equal interests, or possess equal moral status.

The practical commitments are more defensible than the metaphysical shortcut: preserve corrigibility, pluralism, reversibility, contestability, visible dependencies, feasible appeal, and exit or refusal where possible; resist domination; and do not silently destroy nonfungible bearers or relationships merely because they are difficult to classify.

I also add an aggregation guardrail. A million tiny benefits do not automatically justify catastrophic irreversible sacrifice imposed on a minority. And a real higher-scale organization does not automatically inherit subjecthood or direct moral standing from its components, nor do components inherit the standing of the whole.

Responsibility follows control, foreseeability, prevention capacity, role, and authority more directly than it follows substrate.

Why I will not identify the connecting pattern with one iconic surface

The mathematics best suited to organizing my research may not be the mathematics that best describes the physical systems it investigates. My leading implementation candidate is a time-evolving typed evidence graph, possibly supplemented by native hyperedges and context-dependent consistency checks. Hyperbolic embeddings might help navigate some hierarchies. A gyroid may illuminate the topology of particular natural materials. These are different jobs; none is established as the ultimate geometry of reality.

I intend to let the simplest adequate representation compete against richer alternatives. The test is whether it preserves joint premises, alternative support routes, source dependence, local-to-global compatibility, explicit loss conditions, and history under revision; whether it predicts or detects errors better; and what it costs. A diagram that fits many domains without improving independent tests is not an ontological discovery.

The most useful result may be the ability to choose which geometry is appropriate to which explanatory target. A tool for discovering accurate cross-domain mappings would be valuable even if reality never turns out to have a single master geometry. I want the framework's mathematical architecture to become experimentally accountable, not a new emblem protected from criticism.

Information, state spaces, and formal structure require typed ontological bridges

I increasingly treat “information is fundamental” the same way I treat “everything is experience,” “everything is physical,” or “everything is process”: first stabilize the referent.

Statistical information, semantic content, representation, knowledge, and value are different constructs. A Shannon-style measure can quantify uncertainty or correlation without containing semantic aboutness. If someone strips away sender, receiver, meaning, use, and physical carrier, the remaining claim may be about distinguishable states, lawful relations, or constraints rather than information in the ordinary semantic sense.

The same discipline applies to formal spaces. A state space, morphospace, latent space, Hilbert space, or fitness landscape can reveal real structure. Turning that structure into an ontologically independent causal realm requires another bridge: what the formal objects refer to, what makes the domain independent, how it couples to measured systems, and what outcome changes because that stronger interpretation is true.

Useful structure can be real without every useful space becoming a second world.

Mathematical explanation is not yet a Platonic cause

A mathematical truth can be indispensable to explanation, constrain what is possible, and support counterfactual reasoning without becoming an additional entity that reaches into a physical system and pushes it around.

If someone proposes interactionist mathematical causation, I want the causal bridge and the differential consequence. If someone then proposes a separately existing Platonic realm, I want the independence bridge too. The effectiveness of mathematics is a fact to explain, not a blank check for one ontology of mathematics.

Reality can be real at more than one scale

One of my strongest commitments is now that “real” does not mean “fundamental particle.”

Cells are real.

Organisms are real.

Storms are real.

Languages are real.

Markets are real.

Species are real in scientifically useful senses.

Institutions are real.

Selves may be real as dynamically maintained patterns.

Agency can be real at an organismal scale even if every operation is physically implemented by lower-scale processes.

The burden is functional and causal:

Does the higher-scale pattern support robust prediction?

Does intervening on variables at that scale change outcomes in ways not captured as usefully by a rival description?

Does it compress without destroying the relevant structure?

Does it transfer?

Does it survive reasonable changes of representation?

This is why I reject both crude reductionism and lazy holism.

Lower-scale realization does not make the higher scale unreal.

Higher-scale usefulness does not make it a new fundamental substance.

The boundary and scale are hypotheses to be tested.

A self can be real without being an unchanging thing

I no longer think the choice is between a permanent inner object and an illusory self. A person can persist through organized causal inheritance: body, dispositions, relationships, memory traces, goals, habits, records, self-models, and recurrent dynamics can preserve enough continuity for the person-level pattern to remain real while its components change.

That does not settle every puzzle of numerical identity. It does mean process continuity can be genuine identity without numerical persistence of an immutable bearer. The same logic applies, with different criteria, to organisms, institutions, software services, and other dynamically maintained systems.

Determinacy is also scale-relative in a more precise way than I used to say

The word “determinism” is another conceptual trap.

A stochastic model tells me that the model uses a probability distribution for outcomes conditional on its variables. That does not by itself tell me whether reality is fundamentally random.

A deterministic model can work extremely well in a domain without proving universal metaphysical determinism.

And an individual trajectory can be uncertain even when the probability distribution, attractor structure, phenotype, or macrostate is highly constrained.

I therefore now separate several kinds of determinacy:

  • trajectory determinacy,
  • transition-law determinacy,
  • distributional or ensemble determinacy,
  • macrostate determinacy,
  • attractor determinacy,
  • robustness or canalization,
  • causal-control determinacy.

This is what I mean by Local Multiscale Conditional Determinacy.

Conditional determinism is not metaphysical inevitability

There is a further distinction I now want explicit. Let A be a complete actual state at some physically meaningful time, L the relevant laws or transition constraints, D deterministic uniqueness over the domain, and H the resulting history. Then:

A + L + D → exactly one H.

That is conditional uniqueness. It says what follows given A and L. It does not by itself establish that A had to obtain, that L had to obtain, or that H was the only metaphysically possible history. To move from “given these conditions, one history follows” to “these conditions themselves could not have been otherwise” requires an additional modal argument.

A perturbation makes the distinction especially clear. If an admissible alternative state A′ differs only in some variable x → x + δ, and the same laws yield H′ rather than H, then the perturbation shows counterfactual leverage and path dependence. It also shows that L alone does not entail H. If A′ is claimed physically or metaphysically impossible, that impossibility needs its own warrant; conceptual describability alone is not enough to establish possibility.

This also separates determinism from predictability. A deterministic system can be chaotic and practically unpredictable. Failure to predict a trajectory does not establish ontological indeterminism, just as a stochastic model does not by itself establish fundamental randomness.

And it separates causal ancestry from causal leverage. Some perturbations amplify, some wash out, some are buffered, and many distinct microhistories can preserve approximately the same macrostate. The relevant question is not whether an earlier difference exists somewhere in the ancestry, but whether that difference survives to the target boundary, scale, timescale, and outcome.

Dynamics asks what follows from actuality. Modality asks what actuality could have been. First principles ask why there is determinate actuality at all. None should answer the others without an explicit bridge.

The important lesson is not “everything becomes deterministic at a higher scale.”

Sometimes coarse-graining increases useful regularity.

Sometimes it destroys information.

Sometimes it makes a process history-dependent.

Sometimes noise produces transitions that have no deterministic counterpart.

One mathematical case makes the epistemic point especially clear. In literal convolution, an output can aggregate contributions across many compatible decompositions. Depending on the kernel and observation conditions, the inverse problem may be unique, non-unique, or so ill-conditioned that small noise produces large uncertainty about the source. More generally, whenever a measurement or coarse-graining is many-to-one, distinct underlying states or histories can generate the same retained observable. Repeating the same information-losing observation can estimate that observable more precisely without restoring distinctions the channel removed.

This means macro-level regularity and microhistorical recoverability can move in opposite directions. A higher-scale pattern can become more stable, predictable, and scientifically useful while the exact lower-scale provenance becomes less identifiable. Failure to reconstruct a distinction after coarse-graining therefore does not show that the distinction was unreal; conversely, the mere possibility that a lossy channel erased a distinction does not establish that the hidden distinction exists.

I treat convolution here as a bounded mathematical example, not a master ontology. Recursion, feedback, emergence, causation, attention, integration, and cognition are not convolution merely because several influences combine into one result. The analogy earns use only where the relevant composition law, transformation, and loss structure are actually specified.

The point is to ask where the determinacy resides and what kind it is, rather than using “stochastic” as a synonym for metaphysical randomness or “deterministic” as a synonym for inevitable, predictable, or unagentic.

Some truths may be beyond every physically realizable observer

I now treat this as a live possibility rather than science-fiction decoration. Every observer I know how to describe has finite lifetime, finite bandwidth, finite computation, limited causal access, and a location inside the world being investigated. There is no guarantee that every truth about the whole is recoverable from every possible position inside it.

I do not claim that any particular unanswered metaphysical question is permanently unknowable. That would require a much stronger impossibility argument. The narrower conclusion is enough: epistemic incompleteness may be structural rather than merely temporary, so “we do not know” can sometimes be the most mature state of inquiry rather than an embarrassment waiting for a sufficiently confident philosopher.

My current ontology is deliberately thinner than my imagination

I can imagine many stronger pictures.

Maybe Whitehead is right and causal actuality is intrinsically experiential.

Maybe some version of neutral monism is closer.

Maybe idealism wins.

Maybe a future physicalism becomes so well-specified and explanatorily complete that it earns exhaustive ontological status.

Maybe consciousness depends on a biological mechanism not reproduced elsewhere.

Maybe phenomenality occurs in radically different substrates.

Maybe non-necessitating organismic control turns out to be a real causal category.

Maybe something we currently call divine survives every naturalistic decomposition and begins generating distinctive evidence.

The framework does not tell me those things are false because they are unusual.

It tells me to stop giving them evidential credit they have not yet earned.

The current ontology therefore remains:

provisional, selective, boundary- and scale-relative realism plus local agnosticism about stronger intrinsic, universal, identity, and necessity claims where the evidence has not discriminated them.

That is a long phrase for a simple posture:

Reality pushes back. Some patterns clearly survive that pushback. Commit to those patterns at the strength and scale the evidence earns, and be reluctant to turn them into a final metaphysical inventory.

Reality gets veto power over this worldview

Reality’s veto concerns accuracy and warranted revision; corrigibility does not guarantee a true conclusion. A voltage measurement and a promise have different dependence conditions: a promise depends on constituting practices, while its existence need not depend on one person’s present assent. The Ethos distinguishes truth, warrant, usefulness, and authority.

No idea gets protection because I have invested years in it, because it makes the rest of my worldview elegant, because it fits my temperament, or because abandoning it would make the story less satisfying. If repeated discriminating evidence defeats a commitment, the commitment goes.

The reciprocal rule matters just as much. I do not reject a live possibility merely because my current ontology has no comfortable category for it. If reality exceeds the vocabulary, the vocabulary loses.

No protected conclusions. No compulsory mysteries. No final ontology by seniority.

The rival worlds are WΦ = D + Φ and W¬Φ = D + ¬Φ. E? names my present inability to discriminate them adequately. Agnosticism is no longer written as though it were a property of the target.

34. I add metric-calibrated and genealogy-resistant convergence

Representational convergence now earns weight only at the scale and metric where it survives null calibration, causal intervention, and common-cause auditing. Different companies are not automatically independent realizations.

35. I add synthetic perspectivity as a phenomenology-neutral construct

Some computational runtimes now exhibit a real operational perspective for integration, recursive self/world modeling, counterfactual reasoning, and control. I do not equate that with phenomenal subjectivity.

36. I freeze a cross-substrate forecast ledger

I now expect task-conditioned causal convergence, stronger local than global representational convergence, increasing functional valuation without automatic phenomenal valence, improved but non-self-authenticating machine introspection, and continued failure of some substrate-necessity claims for specific capacities. These are prospective bets that can fail.

I do not equate fallible introspection with an absence of experience. I can doubt a report's causal explanation, its interpretation of intrinsic properties, or its reliability in a particular domain without withdrawing every other thing it tells me. Nor do I infer that a computational report has no state-sensitive information because its wording can change with the prompt. My confidence should track the actual bearer, task, controls and comparison. Evolution may help explain why self-representations matter to organisms, but adaptive value alone decides neither their accuracy nor phenomenal constitution. I leave those stronger claims open to appropriate evidence and argument.

37. I strengthen common-cause and gold-standard audits

Agreement among systems that share training culture, architecture, data, benchmarks, or human archetypes is discounted for dependence, and consciousness markers calibrated from human reports are not treated as independent validation of phenomenality unless an additional bridge is supplied.

The shortest version

I think reality is real and that our access to it is partial.

I think scientific and naturalistic explanation has earned extraordinary confidence where it has repeatedly predicted, intervened, replicated, and replaced weaker supernatural explanations.

I do not think that success automatically proves an exhaustive universal physicalism.

I reject many sufficiently specified supernatural and literalist religious claims strongly where the evidence warrants rejection.

I withhold belief from unsupported supernatural additions.

I am methodologically ignostic about God-language whose content remains insufficiently specified, and agnostic about intelligible unresolved claims.

I am agnostic about coherent intrinsic-nature claims where serious rivals remain underdetermined.

I am realist about gods and religions as physically realized historical-social patterns with enormous causal significance.

I think process, relation, organization, and scale matter deeply, but none gets to become the intrinsic nature of everything by vocabulary alone.

I think consciousness is real, but its phenomenal bridge remains unresolved.

I think agency is real as constraint-conditioned, state-sensitive, reachable-state control inside causal history.

I think deterministic dynamics, if established, would fix a history conditional on the relevant state and laws; that would not by itself prove those conditions or that history metaphysically necessary.

I do not currently see evidence for causal sovereignty or self-origination.

I no longer treat “free will” as one thing that has simply been disproven. I decompose it where that preserves the target, compare whole accounts where it does not, and ask what supported difference remains.

I think responsibility, protection, restitution, rehabilitation, and accountability can remain even if basic desert fails.

I treat intense spiritual and mystical experience as real phenomenological evidence whose metaphysical interpretation remains separately corrigible.

I use Hume, Darwin, James, Sagan, Hitchens, Carlin, Whitehead, Dennett, Sapolsky, Nāgārjuna, Rumi, Spinoza, and many others as sources of pressure and insight, not as authorities.

I treat cosmic boundedness as a standing warning against confusing strong local knowledge with final ontology.

I now think the specified causal necessity question raised in the Whitehead dispute has a positive answer at the tested targets without settling the intrinsic nature of causation.

My best consciousness hypothesis emphasizes recursively sustained bounded perspective, integration, temporal continuity, and valuation, while the phenomenal identity remains unresolved.

I distinguish the base model from the operative runtime: M0 ≠ S*. System-level capability may be real without being a property of the weights alone.

I use REI as a candidate schema for comparing episodes of integration with slower inherited constraints. Any invariance claim must specify transformations, preserved relations, domain, and evidence. The schema is not a consciousness criterion. See the methodological specification.

I want corrigible institutions and technologies aimed toward a shareable world without assuming equal status for every possible bearer.

And I treat my own framework the same way.

The question is never simply:

Which grand story do I belong to?

It is:

What exactly is being added, what difference should that addition make, what could show that difference wrong, and exactly what should I change if it does?
