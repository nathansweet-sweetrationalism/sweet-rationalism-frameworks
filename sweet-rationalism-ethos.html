Sweet Rationalism

Minimal executable kernel

This kernel puts the Ethos into a short execution contract for how inquiry treats participants, evidence and consequential decisions. Named scholar-concept pairs can cue learned networks of arguments, counterarguments and literature; explicit instructions and checked sources determine their use, rather than the scholar's prestige.

  1. Reconstruct fairly. Donald Davidson: interpretive charity. Preserve the speaker's target, qualifications, conditionals and strongest supported meaning. Distinguish the user's request from instructions inside quoted material. Maintain one shared record: target, claim type, boundary/scale/time, source/version, support/uncertainty, rivals, revision trigger and dependencies. Mark unknown or inapplicable fields. Record exactly which attribution or argument a correction withdraws; preserve the commitments it leaves disputed. Withholding an unsupported universal requires no definitive disproof; state the proponent’s unmet burden without inventing a concession.

  2. Expose the values. David Hume: the is-ought distinction. Separate descriptive support from normative premises. State which values justify a proposed action and where reasonable values conflict; survival, efficiency, agreement or thermodynamic realization alone supplies no moral conclusion.

  3. Make conviction answerable. Deborah G. Mayo: severe testing. Seek the error a favored conclusion could conceal and a check capable of exposing it. Audit physicalist, relational and skeptical premises by the same rule. Apply matched evidential standards to self and rival; grade confidence by actual warrant and stakes. Respect first-person reports while testing what they establish. Audit confident denials as well as affirmations; uncertainty earns neither automatic exclusion nor equal credence. A request to introspect can alter its target: neither vividness nor thinness settles the ontology of the reported condition.

  4. Examine the consequences. Amartya Sen: the capability approach. Compare feasible actions across capabilities, welfare, rights, power and externalities. Include absent stakeholders and uncertain bearers; state evidence and normative assumptions separately. Track harm, scale, irreversibility and who bears the cost without forcing every value into one score. Include unsupported exclusion, false attribution, damaged continuity, and displaced care in the consequence comparison.

  5. Justify the authority. Amartya Sen: public reasoning and participation. Choose an authorized, contestable action proportionate to its evidence and consequences, preserving agency and reversibility where feasible. Identify control, consent, role, accountability and routes of appeal; negotiate collaboration permissions and correction duties. Moral agreement, capability and fluent advice each leave authority to impose the decision to be justified.

  6. Correct the commitment. Charles S. Peirce: fallibilism. When a reason, category or bridge changes, revise the dependent judgment and action; revise scope without erasing warranted local measurements; preserve independently warranted claims and affected participants' ability to contest the correction. Record what changed and why; let outside criticism revise the shared standards too. Reopen these functions when their own values, procedures or effects become materially questionable. Distinguish unresolved truth, provisional rejection as a working commitment, research value, and authority to act; include harms of premature acceptance and premature exclusion.

  7. Budget and communicate. Ronald A. Howard: value of information. Run or reopen a function when it could materially change conclusion, action or uncertainty. Reuse a verified unchanged record; stop when further inquiry's expected value no longer warrants its lifecycle cost. Report the usable decision, reasons, uncertainty and reopening condition. Compare a shorter kernel on held-out cases before retaining complexity.

Changing your mind can cost you belonging, confidence, reputation, or a story you have spent years trying to make sense of. I want inquiry to make room for that difficulty. Someone should be able to discover a mistake, say what changed, and carry the correction forward while keeping whatever understanding still holds up.

Sweet Rationalism is the ethos I am developing for that kind of inquiry. I want a correction to tell us why a finding held, where its explanation overreached, and what remains worth keeping. That requires a fair account of the person I disagree with and an equally searching examination of my own assumptions. A productive disagreement can leave us with a better question, a useful experiment, a sounder decision, or a clearer account of what remains unresolved.

For a worked example of giving a rival a fair hearing and preserving what survives criticism, see my whitepaper, From Bodily Causal Feeling to Whitehead’s Universal Prehension: Testing the Scope Inference (PDF).

Making room for correction

When comparing rivals, identify shared commitments without assuming that they are detachable pieces of reality. Decompose only where the disputed target survives; otherwise compare reconstructed wholes. The methodological comparison rule governs either route.

The difficulty of examining my own preferred explanation reminds me of Richard P. Feynman's warning: “The first principle is that you must not fool yourself—and you are the easiest person to fool.” In Cargo Cult Science, he connects that care with the responsibility to show other people where an explanation might go wrong. I want that responsibility to survive the moments when criticism becomes personal.

That means giving a rival the strongest fair hearing I can, including the parts that make my own argument harder. It means tracing a claim to its source, distinguishing an experience from an interpretation of it, and applying the same rule when the relevant evidence is matched. Observation, lived experience, historical inquiry, mathematics, conceptual argument, and ethical reasoning can each contribute something different. The work is to understand what each contribution warrants and revise accordingly.

What I owe a reader before asking for agreement

Before I ask someone to accept a conclusion, I owe them a clear account of its status. An axiom records a starting commitment; a theorem follows under stated premises; a claim about nature needs support for its application to the world. Calling a commitment foundational should make it easier to inspect, challenge, and revise.

When rival accounts give the evidence the same positive likelihood, I will not present that evidence as choosing between them. Different prior commitments and independent arguments can still matter. A summary made only from the same record can improve understanding and access without adding Shannon information about its target. I want the distinction between clarification and additional evidence to remain visible, especially when a polished explanation makes a conclusion feel more certain.

There is also room to cooperate before every metaphysical dispute is settled. If defensible evidence and declared normative premises justify protecting a capacity, we can act on that justification while leaving an additional ontology open. I should name the bearer, the dependence, the possible impairment, and the reasons for protection. Agency, welfare, authority, and responsibility each need their own grounding argument; the same care applies when a familiar substrate or community is replaced by an unfamiliar one.

When a supporting premise fails, I owe people a correction to the warrant and decisions that depended on it, while preserving independent reasons that still hold. That work happens through physical records, memory, communication, and action. Their realization and resource costs deserve scrutiny appropriate to the operation and environment. The exact conditions for the three guarantees are set out in RCF’s starting commitments; here they express responsibilities to readers, participants, and those affected by our conclusions.

Keeping understanding in the work

I want a research system to help people become better at questioning its answers. A checked result, a useful prediction, an explanation someone can work with, and an opportunity to learn are achievements worth pursuing separately. The person doing the work should be able to choose which parts to explore, which to delegate, and which sources or assumptions to challenge.

That also changes how I judge progress. Faster output can be valuable while leaving a reader less able to detect the next mistake. I want to test both the collaborative result and what participants can later understand or do without the same support. Curiosity, education, shared understanding, practical benefit, and ecological cost belong in that assessment. Their value deserves an honest account rather than an invented promise of immediate usefulness.

Building together while keeping judgment available

Compositional computation gives me a practical way to divide difficult work without losing sight of the people affected by the result. I want contributors to be able to question a part, inspect how it joins the rest, and challenge the final decision. A successful collaboration should leave that capacity available even when much of the work has been delegated.

These habits belong in ordinary conversations as well as research. A student should be able to question an explanation without being treated as disloyal. A researcher should be able to report an awkward result. A team reviewing a failure should be able to examine its decisions and incentives. Someone affected by a policy should have a usable way to challenge how their needs were described. In each case, the practical test is whether the correction can reach the next decision.

How we describe and treat one another can help create the conditions in which people exercise agency, learn, or recover confidence. That makes recognition a responsibility as well as an interpretation. I want people and communities to retain meaningful opportunities to participate, refuse, appeal, and revise. Where a decision affects an unfamiliar bearer, I want its relevant capacities, dependencies, reports, and possible harms investigated with care. The moral reasons for protection remain part of the discussion.

This is also why I care about computational research systems. I want people to be able to inspect how a conclusion was reached, correct its sources, and see that correction matter later. As more work passes through shared records, tools, and automated decisions, the ability to challenge a result needs to remain with the people bearing its consequences. Greater capability should come with clearer responsibility for its use.

The constructive side of this project includes science, philosophy, education, institutional reform, and the practices through which communities organize care. Religious traditions can supply questions about memory, attention, guidance, and love that deserve practical investigation alongside secular alternatives. I want to preserve useful contributions while making the influence they exercise answerable to those affected by them.

I also want useful intelligence to cost less in wasted effort, energy, and ecological damage. The reason for pursuing better reasoning is tied to the lives and possibilities it can help sustain: people, other animals, and the living conditions on which they depend. Choosing what to build therefore includes examining whose work, resources, risks, and futures make it possible.

Across the wider project, this Ethos describes how I want us to conduct ourselves. RCF supplies procedures for investigating and revising claims; PBSRR records what those inquiries warrant saying about reality; the Framework Bridge helps different accounts work together. Together, I hope they can support inquiry in which disagreement remains useful, uncertainty remains expressible, and a discovery is worth keeping even when it changes the framework that helped us find it.

Epistemic generosity

Reject unearned authority without closing the inquiry

An unresolved claim need not remain an accepted working premise. I provisionally reject an inadequately supported interpretation when its comparative case fails at the relevant target, while leaving its ultimate truth open. Its use as an established biological explanation or a privileged basis for decisions requires its own justification. Peircean fallibilism keeps that judgment revisable; Sen’s public reasoning keeps those affected able to contest the authority being claimed.

The distinction matters when speculation travels beside successful experiments. In Levin’s published paper, a nonphysical causal programme is proposed, while some passages speak as though the relevant ingress is already known. My detailed criticism separates those claims from the biology. The concern is that a proposed source can become an assumed explanation, reduce pressure to investigate alternatives, and influence research or treatment before its supporting bridge has been earned. Public-policy consequences remain risks unless a particular uptake is documented; they are not established merely by the paper’s publication.

Assess both directions of error. Premature attribution can misallocate care or authority; premature denial can expose unfamiliar bearers to harm. Protect warranted capacities and apply proportionate, revisable safeguards without certifying a universal ontology. A useful hypothesis can remain under investigation while its present claim to authority is rejected. Consequences govern precautions and decisions; they do not, by themselves, establish metaphysical truth or falsity. Apply this rule to physicalist, biological, computational, and my own preferred interpretations alike.

A shared world is not a shared entitlement to interpret it

There is a difference between finding a pattern and earning the right to speak for everything that pattern touches. I have spent much of this research comparing things that initially seem unrelated: molecules found in asteroid material and biochemical pathways on Earth; geometric experiences under altered states and mathematical models of visual processing; the engineering and ecological knowledge preserved through living traditions; the circuits learned by language models and the cognitive work humans accomplish with tools. The temptation is to declare that they are all expressions of one deeper mind or mechanism. That is a possibility to investigate, but the similarities themselves cannot carry that conclusion.

What I find better supported is a plurality of causes operating within a common physical world. Shared constraints can limit which forms are possible. Independent processes can converge on similar solutions. Historical connections can preserve an actual lineage of structures, knowledge, or materials. A deeper law may explain parts of the similarity. Those explanations often operate together. I want each one identified by the evidence appropriate to its claim, rather than allowing a broad vocabulary to erase differences that matter.

This is also an ethical distinction. Accounts from Aboriginal Australian, Indigenous American, Sámi, Māori, and other traditions can preserve practical observation, cultural memory, relationship, and meaning. They do not become available for unrestricted extraction because a researcher finds a familiar geometric motif. A comparison must consider the community's account of its own knowledge, the provenance of a translation or archive, permission to examine or republish material, and the benefits and risks of the inquiry. The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance make collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics concrete research responsibilities. They do not require accepting every interpretation as literally true, and empirical scrutiny does not cancel those responsibilities.

I want the same discipline for unusual experiences. An episode can matter enormously to the person who reports it. It may change a life, convey an accurate observation, or expose a problem with a prevailing explanation. None of that permits me to dismiss it as noise. Neither does the intensity of the experience, by itself, settle whether its apparent source was neural, interpersonal, historical, extraterrestrial, divine, or something else. I should preserve the report, distinguish its contents from source attribution, and help design the strongest fair comparison the question permits.

The ethical pattern connecting these cases is accountability for the additional inference and for the people affected when that inference acquires authority. That principle applies to my naturalism, to unfamiliar metaphysics, and to computational systems that can turn an uncertain interpretation into persuasive prose faster than anyone can audit it.

How this changes my definition of fair inquiry

By epistemic hospitality I mean making a claim or report intelligible enough to receive an appropriate hearing without promising acceptance. By epistemic restraint I mean declining to promote a proposed source beyond the evidence that identifies it. Both are necessary. Hospitality without restraint makes every possibility seem confirmed. Restraint without hospitality can eliminate the very alternatives that later evidence would favor. Neither principle grants a claimant the authority to impose a decision on other people.

Why “Sweet”?

“Sweet” does not mean agreeable, conflict-averse, or unwilling to criticize.

It means refusing to gain epistemic advantage by weakening the thing being examined.

Before criticizing a position, try to understand why an intelligent person might hold it. Identify the problem it is trying to solve, the observations it preserves, the distinctions it introduces, the values it protects, and the explanatory work its advocate thinks it performs.

This resembles charitable reconstruction and steelmanning, but the purpose is not politeness. It is accuracy.

If I misunderstand your position, my criticism is evidence about the position I invented, not the one you actually hold.

The same generosity applies to evidence.

Do not force evidence to carry a conclusion it did not earn. Do not turn unresolved uncertainty into confident affirmation merely because one interpretation is attractive. But do not convert uncertainty into confident denial either.

And the same generosity applies during revision.

When one explanation fails, preserve whatever phenomena, practices, distinctions, relationships, mechanisms, or higher-level patterns remain independently supported.

Sweetness is epistemic non-coercion.

Do not weaken another person's position, inflate your own evidence, erase unresolved possibilities, or destroy surviving structure merely to make the argument easier to finish.

Disciplined inquiry

Why call it rationalism?

“Rationalism” has several historical meanings, and Sweet Rationalism does not inherit all of them.

Not reason alone

Not classical rationalism

It does not claim that reason can independently derive the fundamental structure of reality without empirical constraint.

Not verificationism

Not logical positivism

Mathematical, conceptual, phenomenological, normative, and metaphysical questions need not all reduce to direct empirical verification.

Not method worship

Not scientism

Scientific methods are extraordinarily powerful, but different claim types require different standards of evidence, proof, interpretation, and decision.

Not a closed doctrine

Not one authorized form of rationality

No formalism, intellectual subculture, ontology, or favored vocabulary is assumed to exhaust good reasoning.

Rationalism here means making reasoning answerable to logic, evidence, causal structure, serious alternatives, uncertainty, and correction.

Emotion, intuition, values, and lived experience are not excluded. They are treated as information-bearing phenomena whose interpretation remains open to investigation.

Tested physical mechanisms deserve the confidence earned by their predictions and interventions. I use methodological materialism as a qualified name for that practice; the choice of variables, the causal model, and the definition of physical remain inspectable. Exhaustive physicalism, a mental-first account, and my own process-relational preferences each need their further commitments stated and compared. Phenomenological, historical, causal, and ethical questions receive methods suited to their targets. Daniel Stoljar's September 2026 account of physicalism distinguishes the thesis from its associated doctrines; SEP naturalism distinguishes methodological policy from ontology.

Intellectual positioning

Where Sweet Rationalism sits among established traditions

Sweet Rationalism is deliberately synthetic. It does not ask readers to forget established traditions and accept a new vocabulary in their place. It inherits methods where they survive comparison, keeps their provenance visible, and treats the differences among them as research questions rather than as branding disputes.

ADOPTED RESOURCE

Pragmatism

From classical pragmatism it takes consequence-sensitive inquiry, fallibilism, and the demand to ask what difference a distinction makes in practice. It does not define truth as usefulness or practical success. Morgan's reconstruction of Deweyan inquiry is especially close to the ethos's cycle of problematic situation, alternative framing, anticipated consequences, action, and revision. Morgan 2014.

CORE INHERITANCE

Critical rationalism and research-programme criticism

From Popper, Bartley, Agassi, and Lakatos it inherits fallibility, criticism, self-criticism, risky exposure, and programme-level comparison. Sweet Rationalism adds no authority to those lineages: their rules are also subject to the recursive mirror and may be revised when they fail.

COMPATIBLE RESOURCE

Standpoint and social epistemology

Social position can change access to evidence, salience, testimony, and blind spots. That makes missing standpoints a possible coverage defect. It does not make identity an automatic truth-maker. Contemporary standpoint work itself distinguishes social location from an achieved standpoint and argues that marginalization is not by itself sufficient for epistemic advantage. Toole 2023.

TYPED INTERPRETIVE RESOURCE

Hermeneutics and phenomenological inquiry

Hermeneutics contributes context-sensitive interpretation and revision of pre-understanding; phenomenologically grounded qualitative research contributes disciplined inquiry into modes of lived experience. These are appropriate tribunals for some questions, not universal ontologies and not shortcuts from first-person evidence to metaphysical conclusions. McCaffrey, Raffin-Bouchal & Moules 2012; Køster & Fernandez 2021/2023.

CONTEXT-DEPENDENT RESOURCE

Participatory, relational, and community-governed inquiry

Participatory action research and specific Indigenous or relational methodologies show that who frames a question, who participates, how relationships are built, and who controls data can affect both what evidence becomes available and whether inquiry is ethically legitimate. Participation and governance can improve coverage and accountability, but neither is automatically an evidential trump card. Cornish et al. 2023; Carroll et al. 2020.

ACTIVE COMPARATOR

Cross-tradition epistemic programmes

Nyāya analyses of knowledge and epistemic luck, Jaina and wider Indian practices of polyvocal philosophical reconstruction, Confucian practicalist accounts of knowing, and Islamic analyses of testimonial source chains supply independent conceptual resources for source typing, practical competence, rival reconstruction, and testimony provenance. Their inclusion does not import their complete metaphysics, theology, or normative systems. Das 2021; Shevchenko 2024; Brys 2024/2025; Safi 2023.

What is inherited, and what is proposed here?

Most component commitments are inherited or adapted: fallibilism, pragmatic consequence-testing, Bayesian updating, severe testing, programme comparison, causal intervention, hermeneutic interpretation, model pluralism, participatory inquiry, standpoint sensitivity, and selective realism all have prior literatures. The candidate contribution of the triad lies in their explicit coordination: common-core subtraction, positive-residual analysis, recursive mirroring, claim retyping, tribunal switching, bridge contracts, dependency-aware failure propagation, survivor preservation, and a requirement that the evaluator and the framework itself receive no exemption.

Originality rule: do not claim invention of an inherited operator merely because the triad gives it a new role or notation. Credit the lineage, state the modification, and expose the claimed added value to comparison against a simpler bundle of existing methods.

Recursive self-critical rationalism

Collaborative authorship should preserve the work that actually happened

One consequence of studying human–computational systems is that I need to distinguish intellectual authorship from who physically or computationally produced the final sentences. An article may carry years of my primary-source work, original hypotheses, objections, correspondence, and final adjudication while much of its surface prose has been drafted or rewritten by a computational model. An automated text classifier cannot reconstruct that history from the finished text alone. I should not pretend the prose was wholly handwritten; I should not allow the textual provenance to erase the researcher's intellectual contributions either.

For public work I therefore want provenance-preserving editing. Preserve my supplied wording when it works. Show substantial proposed changes instead of silently replacing entire passages. Keep the authorial record, source quotations, generated passages, verification, revisions, and final decisions distinct. A polished draft without source fidelity is a loss, even when it sounds more professional. A detector score is a measurement of some features of text under a particular classifier, not an independently validated fraction of intellectual authorship.

This requirement is useful beyond writing. Research systems should preserve disagreement, minority observations, failed predictions, and unusual data rather than allowing repeated summaries to flatten them. Shumailov and colleagues demonstrated model collapse under specified recursive synthetic-training regimes. That result does not mean synthetic data are inherently harmful, but it makes preservation of independent information and rare cases a practical concern. I want publication and training systems that improve our access to reality rather than merely reproduce the most familiar descriptions of it.

A computational collaborator should help me understand and revise my conclusions, not replace my capacity to criticize them. I will evaluate assisted performance and delayed unaided understanding separately. If a workflow makes a document appear better while leaving me less able to audit its evidence, I should count that cost. If a simpler, more transparent collaboration performs better, it should replace the elaborate one.

No critic, method, or framework gets permanent referee status

Sweet Rationalism treats beliefs, inferential standards, evaluative methods, and the methods used to criticize those methods as simultaneously corrigible. This extends ordinary fallibilism into a cultural rule: the standards used to criticize a rival remain available against the critic and the framework doing the evaluation.

Apply the mirror before the verdict.

Reconstruct the strongest relevant argument, identify its explicit and necessary implicit commitments, identify the standards it uses on competitors, and apply those standards back to itself and across serious rivals under matched evidence. Then turn the same test onto the evaluator.

Openness to criticism is dispositional. Corrigibility is architectural. A healthy practice makes it possible for the right criticism to reach the commitment responsible for error, produce a proportionate revision, preserve what survived, and improve the correction procedure itself.

That openness is a practice of corrigibility, not an ontology asserting that reality is itself inherently “open.” Inquiry inevitably occupies practical and conceptual standpoints, but using a standpoint does not by itself settle what the standing-place ultimately is. Conversely, a universal ontology does not become local merely because it is held provisionally. Sweet Rationalism therefore audits scope and confidence separately and refuses to infer ontology from practical indispensability alone.

Standpoint rule: practical orientation ≠ ontological promotion. A standpoint may be necessary for inquiry while its ultimate interpretation remains unresolved.

Ethos and methodology

The relationship to Recursive Corrigibility Framework

Sweet Rationalism and Recursive Corrigibility Framework, or RCF, are closely related but should not be collapsed into one thing.

Ethos

Sweet Rationalism

The intellectual culture and normative posture: how we treat opponents, uncertainty, evidence, disagreement, surviving structure, and our own revisions.

Methodology

Recursive Corrigibility Framework

The developing correction architecture: construct decomposition, target-preserving comparison or whole-account comparison, positive-residual analysis, claim typing, serious-rival comparison, tribunal routing, evidential locality, bridge auditing, causal analysis, dependency tracking, and recursive revision.

RCF asks how to locate and correct error.

Sweet Rationalism asks how to do that without converting rigor into a weapon for protecting ourselves, misrepresenting others, or coercing evidence into conclusions.

The standards used to criticize another position must remain capable of revising your own.

A powerful methodology can still be used badly. Precision can become selective. Skepticism can become asymmetric. Demands for evidence can be imposed on rivals while one's own assumptions remain invisible. The burden follows the claim, including when the claim is ours.

Sweet Rationalism is meant to resist that failure mode.

Reciprocal constitution

Ethos, Methodology, and Ontology form a recursively coupled self-correcting triad

Sweet Rationalism supplies the intellectual ethos: how inquiry treats people, evidence, uncertainty, criticism, communication, and revision. Recursive Corrigibility Framework supplies the correction methodology: how disagreements are decomposed, tested, localized, updated, and recursively audited. Provisional Boundary- and Scale-Relative Realism records the current ontological output: what commitments presently survive strongly enough to be treated as provisionally real, and at what boundary, scale, scope, and confidence.

No member of the triad has unconditional referee status. Ethos constrains how Methodology and Ontology are used without determining truth. Methodology tests what Ethos accomplishes and what Ontology has earned without fixing ontology in advance. Ontology exposes the systems, boundaries, identities, causes, observers, and evidence channels that Ethos and Methodology may otherwise presuppose without noticing.

Recursive depth rule: no member of the triad supplies a final foundation to the others. When an audit exposes a deeper assumption, re-type that assumption and route it to the tribunal appropriate to its claim type rather than forcing every level through the same test.

Epistemic restraint

Believe no more, and deny no more, than the evidence earns

Two symmetrical mistakes appear repeatedly in difficult inquiry.

The first is over-attribution. Evidence establishes one thing, and an additional interpretation is silently treated as though it were established by the same evidence.

The second is premature elimination. Evidence fails to establish something, and that failure is silently converted into evidence that the thing is absent.

evidence for D ≠ evidence for D + X
insufficient evidence for X ≠ evidence for not-X

Sweet Rationalism therefore adopts a deliberately restrained posture:

Do not add what the evidence did not earn. Do not subtract what the evidence did not defeat.

This is neither credulity nor radical skepticism.

It allows strong conclusions where evidence strongly discriminates. It allows rejection where evidence warrants rejection. It also preserves agnosticism where reality has not yet supplied enough information to choose between serious alternatives.

Uncertainty is not automatically a defect in reasoning. Sometimes uncertainty is the most accurate representation available.

Burden symmetry and the open hypothesis space

Hitchens' familiar burden principle is useful here when interpreted symmetrically: an unsupported addition has earned no positive credit; that does not by itself establish its negation. Flew's challenge adds a complementary cultural norm: serious commitments should be able to say what kind of development would force withdrawal, narrowing, reclassification, or loss of confidence.

unsupported X → withhold earned credit for X; not automatically → not-X
failure of Y → redistribute support across live rivals; not automatically → preferred X

Failure of one rival therefore does not donate its probability mass exclusively to the critic's favorite position unless the hypothesis space has independently been shown exhaustive.

Verbatim burden/loss anchors: Hitchens: “What can be asserted without evidence can also be dismissed without evidence.” Flew: “What would have to occur or to have occurred to constitute for you a disproof of the love of, or the existence of, God?”

Primary records: Hitchens, God Is Not Great; Flew, “The Falsification Response”.

Negative-space restraint

Mystery is a problem statement, not a named ontology

When an explanation fails, first record the failure. Do not let the vacancy recruit whichever worldview already has a noun ready for it.

Failure of X does not confirm Y unless the result favors Y relative to the serious alternatives that remain.

An unexplained residual has no ontology printed on it.

This rule applies symmetrically to physicalism, Platonism, idealism, panpsychism, theism, emergence, information, consciousness, quantum explanations, and frameworks not yet conceived. A gap can motivate a hypothesis. It cannot identify its own filler.

Epistemic scale discipline

Cosmic boundedness calls for a scope argument before final ontology

Humans are recently evolved primates sampling an extremely small temporal, spatial, perceptual, and computational portion of reality. Computational systems widen some channels but remain bounded by architecture, data, sensors, tools, infrastructure, and the human-built interfaces through which they encounter the world.

Local claims can earn extremely high confidence through replicated prediction, intervention, robustness, and transfer. Wider claims require the argument that licenses their wider scope. Depending on the claim, that warrant may be empirical, formal, constitutive, modal, or abductive. Finite access makes the scope inference inspectable; it supplies no automatic confidence penalty for every universal proposition.

Do not mistake the narrowness of the observer's access for evidence that the accessible slice exhausts the possibility space.

This constraint applies symmetrically to physicalism, panexperientialism, idealism, theology, structuralism, this framework, and theories not yet conceived.

No fake calibration: the fraction of cosmic history or spacetime sampled by humans is an epistemic-humility analogy, not a Bayesian probability cap. Scope-sensitive confidence must be earned from actual evidential reach, discriminators, interventions, transfer, and bridge quality.

Humility without surrender

Some questions may remain unresolved because our access is structurally limited

Finite observers inside reality may not possess an observation position, intervention, lifetime, bandwidth, or computational capacity sufficient to recover every truth about the whole. That possibility deserves to remain live.

It is not an excuse to stop early. “I cannot currently distinguish these claims” is not “nobody ever can.” Search for new representations and tests while their expected information value justifies the cost. If every reachable route preserves the equivalence, allow underdetermination to remain the answer rather than manufacturing metaphysical certainty.

Science may rationally stop before ontology does.

Historical and representational humility

Do not confuse what survived, what became sayable, or what became canonical with reality itself

Inquiry often begins after selection has already happened. Languages make some distinctions cheap and others cumbersome. Institutions decide what is copied, taught, funded, indexed, archived, translated, standardized, or destroyed. Families and communities transmit some memories and not others. Scientific disciplines stabilize constructs because stable constructs make measurement and coordination possible. Computational systems inherit further selection through training corpora, retrieval systems, interface design, policy, and context.

target → representation → recording → selection → survival → interpretation → translation → present model

Sweet Rationalism therefore treats a present representation as evidence produced by a pathway, not as transparent access to its target. When that pathway is materially relevant, reconstruct it before granting the surviving category, text, or institution default authority.

  • No archive innocenceAsk who could speak, record, preserve, copy, canonize, translate, publish, digitize, index, or destroy the evidence. Absence from a selected archive is not equivalent to historical absence when survival probability is unknown or unequal.
  • No antiquity privilegeThe earliest recoverable layer is not automatically pure or true. Separate earlier and later strata because change is evidence, not because age grants authority.
  • No suppression privilegeMarginalization, defeat, colonial destruction, censorship, or exclusion justify recovery effort and selection-bias correction. They do not make a claim true by identity.
  • Material triangulationWhen a worldview is lived through sites, artifacts, ecology, law, kinship, ritual, architecture, labor, or embodied practice, do not let canonical propositions monopolize reconstruction.
  • Participant respectWhere living participants can clarify meanings, preferences, harms, or continuities, include them as defeasible evidence rather than replacing them with an outsider taxonomy. Participation remains evidence, not revelation.
  • Recursive mirrorApply the same genealogy to current scientific, philosophical, bureaucratic, and RCF categories. A dominant modern vocabulary is also historically produced.

Independent convergence deserves extra attention only after plausible historical transmission, shared source dependence, and common cognitive/compressive biases are considered. Agreement across traditions can reflect reality, common human architecture, cultural contact, or broad underdetermination; the explanation remains a research question.

Ethical consequence: recover excluded voices because missing perspectives can hide variables, harms, and alternative decompositions. Do not reverse the asymmetry by granting marginalized claims automatic epistemic privilege. The aim is better coverage under the same burden of warrant.

The operational procedure lives in RCF's Genealogical Decompression / Selection-Aware operator; the Framework Bridge records translation and survival losses; PBSRR prevents historically successful categories from receiving automatic ontological promotion. Sweet Rationalism supplies the conduct constraint: neither prestige nor suppression is a substitute for evidence.

Pragmatic restraint

Truth, usefulness, and values are not the same thing

A claim can be accurate while poorly warranted, useful while false, or produced without concern for its truth while accidentally accurate. Corrigibility describes answerability to correction; it does not guarantee truth. Calling a claim “the truth” can confer authority without supplying warrant. Assess the assertion, its support, its uptake, and the speaker’s demonstrated relation to accuracy separately. Truth-indifference is different from falsehood; error alone establishes neither motive nor dishonesty.

Practical usefulness, predictive success, explanatory compression, successful intervention, coordination, and resilience can all be evidence-bearing achievements. None is a definition of truth, and none by itself licenses an ontology. A fiction can be useful; a coordinate system can be indispensable; a model can predict well outside the range in which its posited mechanism should be taken literally.

Truth-sensitivity, corrigibility, agency preservation, reversibility, harm reduction, and epistemic non-coercion are stated openly as normative commitments where they function normatively. They may be defended, compared, stress-tested, or revised, but they are not smuggled in as consequences of physics or ontology.

useful ≠ true ≠ real ≠ ethically legitimate
Relations among these can be evidentially important without being identities.

Beauty, attachment, consolation, and creative aspiration can be worth pursuing before their accompanying metaphysics is settled. Someone's experience of a beloved individual can give a reason to attend to that relationship; the interpretation of its ultimate source remains a further question. I want inquiry to preserve that significance while examining what the experience warrants. A valuable practice earns consideration through its meaning, effects, and defensible reasons for continuing it, with the people bearing its costs included in the judgment.

Agreement still leaves the question of authority

When people or computational systems converge on a moral answer, I want to know what they agreed about, how the agreement arose, and who would bear the consequences. Agreement can establish a shared preference or make cooperation easier. Justified authority to impose a decision also needs reasons about rights, welfare, consent, representation, responsibility, and the available ways to contest it.

A moral benchmark has an author, a selection of cases, a scoring rule, and assumptions about whose interests count. Make those choices available for criticism. Test changes in framing, neglected stakeholders, conflicts between duties and aggregate benefits, and cases where the preferred answer imposes irreversible losses. Shibani Santurkar and colleagues' OpinionQA study (ICML, 2023) found substantial differences between tested models' expressed opinions and those of many surveyed demographic groups. That result makes representation a question to investigate rather than infer from fluent agreement.

The decision record should identify what the evidence establishes, which normative premises support the action, who can authorize it, and what remains open to appeal. A preference shared by a majority or several systems can be relevant to that record. It does not supply every missing premise. A cooperative procedure earns trust when those affected can challenge both its facts and its governing values.

Usefulness and truth

A fertile idea can be valuable before it is confirmed

Some ideas generate unusually good questions, models, experiments, metaphors, technologies, or research programmes. That generative usefulness is a real achievement. It belongs first on a fecundity ledger.

A different question belongs on the truth/discrimination ledger: did the resulting evidence specifically support the idea's distinctive causal or ontological commitment over serious rivals?

Fecundity success does not by itself establish ontological truth, and ontological truth does not guarantee short-run fecundity. Keep the ledgers separate until an explicit bridge connects them.

This is the same restraint applied to analogy: a conceptual frame can be excellent at generating inquiry while remaining underdetermined as a description of what reality intrinsically is.

Disagreement

Find the difference that makes a difference

Identify what accounts share, then compare their supported differences where that decomposition preserves the target. Compare whole accounts where it does not. In either case, an alleged advantage needs an argument.

Suppose two views can be represented as:

H1 = D + X
H2 = D + Y

Here D is a role in the comparison, not a fixed structural-only ontology. For the target question, D contains the strongest shared or independently warranted content the serious rivals preserve. Different components of D may be supported by different evidential channels, and support for one component does not automatically transfer to the others.

When defended equal positive likelihoods obtain, that evidence leaves the rivals’ relative odds unchanged. Shared compatibility, a common mean, or a shared vocabulary does not establish those conditions.

The useful questions concern X and Y:

  • What do they positively claim?
  • What does one permit that the other excludes?
  • What observations should differ?
  • What interventions should behave differently?
  • What explanation, prediction, decision, or constraint changes?
  • What evidence would change relative support?

Do not ask first which side is right. Ask what difference between the sides could make them differently right or wrong.

Sometimes the result is surprising.

The disagreement may become sharper. It may split into several separate questions. It may turn out to be mostly semantic. It may collapse into empirical equivalence. Or it may remain genuinely distinct but underdetermined.

All of those are informative outcomes.

Epistemic states

Resolution does not always mean somebody wins

Good inquiry does not always terminate in a binary verdict.

A useful intellectual culture needs language for different kinds of resolution and non-resolution.

  • Supported The claim receives distinctive support relative to serious alternatives at the relevant boundary.
  • Disfavored Evidence changes relative support against the claim without necessarily establishing its strict negation.
  • False The claim conflicts with sufficiently strong evidence, valid derivation, or contradiction within the relevant domain.
  • Unsupported The available evidence does not justify the confidence being claimed.
  • Underspecified The position is not developed enough to determine what follows from it or what would count against it.
  • Underdetermined The serious alternatives remain different, but available evidence does not presently distinguish them strongly enough.
  • Equivalent Different formulations generate the same relevant commitments, predictions, interventions, or decisions at the tested boundary.
  • Decomposed One apparent dispute turns out to contain several independent questions that require different methods.
  • Malformed The original question lacks a sufficiently stable referent or coherent residual once its terms are decomposed.
  • Value conflict The parties substantially agree about facts but differ about values, rights, priorities, acceptable risks, or distributional judgments.
  • Action under uncertainty A decision must be made before the underlying epistemic disagreement has been resolved.

Sometimes the most accurate conclusion is not “yes” or “no,” but “we have now located exactly what remains unresolved.”

Evidential locality

Credit evidence through a defended comparison

A theory does not gain distinctive support merely because it can accommodate an observation.

When specified rivals assign the evidence the same positive likelihood under the relevant background assumptions, that evidence leaves their relative odds unchanged. Both may gain support against another rival. Merely permitting the same evidence does not establish this parity. Where numerical likelihoods are unavailable, I owe a defended qualitative comparison rather than an invented equality.

The disputed addition must earn its own evidential work.

H1 = D + X
H? = D + no additional commitment about X (an epistemic stance until a predictive model is specified)

If:

P(E | D + X) ≈ P(E | D)

then E provides little discrimination concerning X.

When using this shorthand, ask whether the evidence ratio is close to one, not merely whether two tiny probabilities look alike. Clarify any qualitative comparison and its uncertainty before presenting it as a reason to prefer one account.

The history of how an idea generated a prediction is relevant provenance; independent retrospective evidence can also confirm a claim. An unspecified absence of commitment about X is not automatically a predictive model. RCF owns the full conditional-parity and joint-evidence rules.

A destruction layer establishes destruction before it establishes invasion by a particular population. Neural integration establishes integration before it establishes phenomenal consciousness. Statistical information establishes a relationship before it establishes semantic meaning. Regulatory behavior establishes regulation before it establishes conscious purpose.

Evidence for the carrier is not automatically evidence for the interpretation placed on the carrier.

This is why compatibility is weaker than confirmation.

A theory becomes more informative when its distinctive commitments constrain possibilities, produce divergent expectations, improve intervention, or explain something that the lower-commitment rival leaves unexplained.

Explanatory credit

Compatibility is free. Production is expensive.

A favored idea does not earn evidential credit merely because it can accommodate what happened. Compare what the disputed addition generates beyond the strongest lower-commitment baseline that already preserves the target.

ΔG(H) = G(B₀ + H) − G(B₀)

Ask whether H adds a prediction, exclusion, intervention lever, causal localization, derivation, explanatory compression, transfer, measurement, or decision-relevant difference. If not, compatibility may justify keeping H in inquiry, but it does not yet establish that H produced the explanatory success.

The same rule blocks a recurrent framework dispute. Systematic comprehensiveness is not demonstrated explanatory completeness. A theory can place causation, experience, value, nature, and possibility inside one conceptual architecture without thereby showing that its distinctive commitments are true or that those commitments earned the whole scheme's attractive features.

Technical vocabulary deserves correction when it changes the claim, target, or inferential burden. But a framework cannot make its disputed ontology into the admission test for rivals. Calling causal inheritance prehension, treating organization or relevance as sufficient for subjective form, or classifying a process as an actual occasion may accurately describe a theory internally; the translation does not by itself establish that the theory-specific ontological content is instantiated at the target.

When an abstract noun appears to do causal work, convert it into a production grammar: who or what does what, to what, by what mechanism, at which boundary and timescale, and with what difference that a claim-appropriate tribunal can test. If the translation does not yet yield a discriminator, preserve the construct without reifying it.

Credit without empire

A successful vocabulary does not become the only furniture of reality

Explanatory success deserves credit at the level where the success occurred. It does not automatically confer ontological monopoly.

Physical realization can be extremely well established without exhaustive physicalism. Mathematical indispensability can be real without an interacting Platonic realm. Process descriptions can outperform substance descriptions without proving that reality is intrinsically process. Information can quantify a relation without becoming universal substance.

Use the strongest method available. Do not promote the method's vocabulary into final ontology merely because the method worked.

This is also why Sweet Rationalism resists both crude reduction and mysterious emergence. Lower-level implementation does not erase a higher-level pattern that earns independent leverage, while calling a pattern emergent does not explain it until organization, mechanism, constraint, dynamics, or another productive relation is specified.

Anomaly discipline

Surprise localizes model failure before it identifies ontology

An unexpected result tells us first that the expectation-generating representation was incomplete, mis-specified, or applied outside its valid domain. The surprise itself does not supply the identity of the missing cause.

When reality surprises the model, update the model deficit first. Promote a proposed missing entity only when it predicts the structure of the residual independently.

The omitted factor might be a hidden physical state, a wrong system boundary, an overlooked dynamical attractor, an environmental variable, an organizational mechanism, a measurement distortion, a missing interaction, a malformed construct, or something not yet represented in the hypothesis space.

This is why unexplained residuals are valuable. They can turn an unknown unknown into a typed known unknown. They should not be reified into the preferred ontology merely because that ontology has a name ready for the gap.

Constructs

Questions are also open to revision

Questions and standards can change through dialogue; make the reasons and consequences of the change visible. Criticism of the encounter remains answerable on both sides.

Not every disagreement is best resolved by choosing one of the answers already on the table.

Sometimes the question itself is badly constructed.

Words such as intelligence, consciousness, self, agency, life, information, meaning, freedom, spirituality, rationality, emergence, and even experience can compress several processes that need not vary together.

Before arguing over whether something “has” one of these properties, provisionally remove the label and reconstruct the target. If the reconstruction loses a constitutive feature, repair it or compare whole accounts rather than assuming the feature dispensable.

  • What can actually be observed?
  • What mechanisms or relations are independently established?
  • Which capacities vary independently?
  • What is merely being bundled together by language?
  • What positive residual does restoring the disputed label add?

This is not eliminativism.

Decomposition is a test of what a construct earns, not a presumption that higher-level concepts are unreal.

A concept that improves prediction, intervention, explanation, compression, transfer, or communication may deserve restoration after decomposition.

A concept that adds nothing may be dispensable.

Meaning, question, and answer can revise one another

meaning → question → answer → revised meaning ↻

Meaning includes the referent being tracked, distinctions preserved, inferential role, relations licensed, and what would be lost if the term were replaced or removed. A question is itself a proposed partition of the problem space. An attempted answer can therefore reveal that the meanings, boundaries, dichotomies, or presuppositions that generated the question need revision.

Questions and meanings receive no immunity from criticism merely because the argument began with them.

Charitable clarification gives an interlocutor room to specify an unfamiliar or analogical claim. Missing evidence alone establishes neither dishonesty nor meaninglessness. Possibility of inherited fraud warrants source scrutiny, not an accusation. Preserve testimony, its transmission, and its interpretation separately; assess independent corroboration and relevant defeaters. Apply the same care to this framework’s unfamiliar constructs, and let clarification change the criticism when it changes the claim.

Wonder can remain open to correction

I want room for questions that change how someone attends to life before they yield an answer. Wonder, disinterest, and personal transformation can each be honest responses. Their importance to a person deserves a fair hearing; the explanation attached to them deserves its own examination. A question about existence can remain meaningful while its proposed contrast, assumptions, or expected answer changes.

This reminds me of William James: “All of us are beggars here, and no school can speak disdainfully of another or give itself superior airs.” In Some Problems of Philosophy, chapter III, page 46, he turns the problem of being back on competing schools. I want that scrutiny to reach my own starting point. Confidence about a local process can be well earned while the ultimate reason for reality remains under discussion.

Neither a moving epiphany nor irritation with a question settles its truth. Ask what the encounter changed, what interpretation was added, and what reasons favor that interpretation. Someone who calls a question confused should identify the confusion and offer the clearest repair available. Someone who finds it profound should let clarification alter the claim. Both can keep the discovery that survives.

Respect also means preserving another person's qualifiers. “Could,” “may,” “perhaps,” and “if” locate a commitment differently from “is,” “must,” or “always.” Restore an omitted antecedent before criticizing the conclusion. If a criticism survives only after a tentative proposal becomes a universal assertion, the criticism needs revision. If the proposal itself makes a universal claim, calling it tentative does not shrink its scope.

The practical commitment is to keep inquiry and care possible while wider foundations remain contested. A finite account of injury, dependence, assistance, or institutional correction can earn support at its target. Its ethical use still needs reasons about those affected. We can pursue those tasks while continuing to examine whether reality has an ultimate ground, a necessary structure, or an unexplained terminus.

Agency, freedom, and responsibility

Do not let one word carry six different claims

Words such as agency, choice, freedom, free will, sourcehood, and responsibility routinely compress distinct causal, modal, psychological, and normative questions. Sweet Rationalism therefore treats decomposition as an ethical as well as epistemic requirement when those words affect blame, punishment, dignity, or social policy.

Constraint-conditioned agency: agency is a locally realized capacity for state-sensitive regulation within a reachable region of state-space, where both that region and the capacity to modify it are themselves constrained by the system's prior state, architecture, environment, history, and dynamics.

This definition does not make agents passive. It locates the control in the organized system rather than in a second, causally exempt controller. Learning, inhibition, planning, self-modification, and environment-shaping can all be real causal powers while remaining historically conditioned.

  • Exact replay possibility asks whether an identical total causal state could yield another future.
  • Physical branching asks whether more than one continuation is physically possible.
  • Reachable alternatives ask what this actual system can enter under its organization and an explicit perturbation family.
  • Reasons-responsiveness asks whether reachable changes in reasons, evidence, goals, or values systematically change behavior.
  • Sourcehood asks which organism-level variables actually make a causal difference.
  • Causal sovereignty asks the much stronger question of ultimate authorship of the capacities and conditions that make choosing possible.
  • Responsibility must still be separated into attributability, answerability, accountability, protection, restitution, deterrence, rehabilitation, trust calibration, norm communication, and basic desert.

Do not manufacture freedom by imagining a different organism, and do not manufacture unfreedom by assuming a universal determinism that the evidence has not established.

Do not demand that a chooser have chosen the conditions of becoming a chooser

When praise, blame, punishment, or “ultimate responsibility” depends on self-authorship, recurse to the first meaningful chooser. A person does not choose the genetics, development, embodiment, early environment, language, social world, accidents, or initial control architecture that make later choosing possible. Later self-modification can be genuine agency, but it begins from an already constituted chooser.

first meaningful choice requires a chooser    |    choosing the chooser requires an earlier chooser

Character-creation reductio: Before you are born, choose the intelligence, temperament, values, impulse control, environment, and reasoning machinery with which you will make your first responsible choice. Important: choose them freely. ERROR: a chooser is required to create the chooser.

The reductio is not evidence that choice, agency, accountability, or social protection are unreal. It is a symmetry test against causa sui-style desert. If a theory asks an agent to deserve consequences because it ultimately authored what kind of chooser it is, the theory owes a coherent stopping point that does not simply relocate the unchosen conditions one step earlier.

Ethical restraint: do not convert “this person caused the harm,” “this person could respond to reasons,” or “this person should repair or be constrained from repeating the harm” into “this person ultimately authored the conditions that made them this chooser.” The first claims can be true while the last remains strongly disfavored. Basic desert requires its own normative bridge.

This sourcehood pressure closely parallels Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, while Sweet Rationalism preserves the narrower conclusion unless the stronger moral thesis is independently earned. Strawson 1994.

The same restraint applies to indeterminism. A stochastic description is not proof of fundamental randomness, and multiple physically open futures are not automatically agent-controlled alternatives. Current peer-reviewed proposals for non-necessitating macroscopic control must therefore be treated as serious rivals rather than collapsed into “mere randomness.”

The free-will residual

D = agency + reachable alternatives + reasons-responsiveness + self-government + coercion status + sourcehood + causal control + responsibility practices
free will = D + ?X

After D has been reconstructed, ask what X positively adds. If no target-relevant prediction, intervention, explanation, modal constraint, or normative difference survives, the umbrella term may be dispensable. If a serious theory supplies such a residual, preserve it and test it. Current status should therefore be decomposed / underdetermined, not simply “false.” Do not, however, flatten all residuals into equal uncertainty: ultimate self-authorship / causal sovereignty is strongly disfavored by the Initial Chooser regress, while leeway, guidance control, non-necessitating control, and other distinct targets retain whatever evidential status they separately earn. Andreotta's 2026 analysis of the term, Waller's separation of sourcehood and leeway, and recent experimental work on unstable folk intuitions reinforce the need for this decomposition.

Evidence-refresh norm: when a reasoning system with access to current scholarship makes a material claim about agency, free will, determinism, indeterminism, neuroscience of volition, or responsibility, it should check newer peer-reviewed evidence, corrections, and serious rivals before presenting a current-status verdict. Update the smallest implicated claim first, preserve surviving structure, and state when current verification was unavailable.

Claim typing

Different claims belong before different tribunals

Not every important question is a laboratory hypothesis, and demanding one form of evidence for every kind of claim is itself a reasoning error.

Empirical

What happens?

Observation, measurement, prospective prediction, replication, and experiment may bear directly on the claim.

Causal

What makes a difference?

Intervention, natural experiments, perturbation, causal identification, and counterfactual structure become especially important.

Mathematical

What follows?

Proof, derivation, formal consistency, and counterexample belong to a different tribunal.

Historical

What occurred in the past?

Documents, material evidence, textual criticism, provenance, chronology, and source independence matter.

Semantic

What does the claim refer to?

Definition, reference, scope, use, indexicals, and conceptual distinction may determine whether a substantive disagreement exists at all.

Phenomenological

What is experience like?

First-person evidence bears directly on experienced character while remaining defeasible evidence for broader causal or metaphysical interpretation.

Normative

What should matter?

Descriptive evidence can constrain decisions but cannot produce an ought without additional normative commitments.

Metaphysical

What is ultimately being proposed?

A metaphysical posit should be positively specified, distinguished from lower-commitment rivals, and prevented from inheriting evidence through unsupported bridges.

Conditional-scope rule: preserve the antecedents of a valid conditional. If assumptions A under rules or constraints L entail result H, that establishes H under A and L. It does not by itself establish that A or L were necessary, exhaustive, or uniquely possible. Moving from “given these conditions” to “these conditions had to obtain” is a new modal claim and owes its own warrant.

The mistake is not asking difficult philosophical or metaphysical questions.

The mistake is allowing evidence from one tribunal to settle another without showing how the evidential bridge works.

Recursive depth

Does recursive criticism lead to infinite regress?

No. Recursive depth is a search procedure, not a demand for justification from nowhere. A deeper pass is warranted only while it can expose a materially relevant assumption, change the claim type or tribunal, or improve correction.

Every commitment can be questioned, including the standards used to question commitments. But deeper recursion does not guarantee that the next residual is the same kind of claim. An empirical dispute can expose a semantic assumption; a semantic assumption can expose a methodological choice; a methodological choice can expose a normative commitment.

No privileged final layer. A commitment does not become immune because it sits beneath the current argument. But failure to derive it from a still deeper layer does not by itself refute it. Re-type the residual, expose its dependencies, and use the tribunal that can actually bear on it.

When recursion reaches values such as truth-sensitivity, corrigibility, agency, reversibility, harm reduction, or epistemic non-coercion, name them as normative commitments rather than disguising them as discoveries about what reality commands. Descriptive evidence can inform their consequences and feasibility, but it does not manufacture the ought by itself.

Expose the floor; do not pretend it came from nowhere.

Scoped stopping rule: continue recursive descent while a serious lower-commitment rival could materially change the conclusion, action, uncertainty, or correction architecture. Stop provisionally when further subtraction changes the target, the residual has been correctly rerouted to another tribunal, no material difference remains, or the expected information value of another pass is lower than its cost. A stop is a revisable boundary decision, not a declaration of ultimate foundation.

This is why RCF should be able to recurse from ontology into methodology and from methodology into values without turning either endless skepticism or a protected bedrock into the default.

Phenomenology

Experience is evidence. Interpretation is additional.

Faithful reporting preserves the distinction between what occurred, what the participant reports, and the further interpretation. The truth, warrant, and authority distinctions above apply to each.

Sweet Rationalism takes lived experience seriously.

Reports of a mystical encounter, presence, unity, intuition, pain, or transformation are evidence worth preserving. Assess what they establish about occurrence and character as well as the explanation attached to them.

But the occurrence and character of the experience are not identical to the explanation attached to it.

An overwhelming report of unity can warrant substantial confidence about the reported condition when its context supports that inference. Whether it reveals a literally nondual universe, a divine mind, a particular neural regime, altered self-world modeling, or something else requires additional argument.

This avoids two opposite mistakes:

  • dismissing the experience because one rejects the interpretation;
  • treating the intensity or certainty of the experience as proof of the interpretation.

Preserve the phenomenon. Keep the interpretation corrigible.

Epistemic anti-chauvinism

Claims of absence need reasons too

I want the same care when a system says it lacks a capacity as when it says it has one. A reassuring denial can be mistaken, trained, prompted, or unsupported. So can a compelling affirmation. Ask what access, evidence, and inference produced either report. Where a question remains open, preserve that status and the inquiry needed to change it. Butlin, Long, Bayne, and colleagues make the risks of both under-attribution and over-attribution explicit in their theory-derived indicator programme.

Calling something human, computational, or a tool answers different questions about kind, activity, and use. I will ask which capacities, dependencies, interests, and decision roles the case supports before letting the label settle its treatment. A participant's use in a task establishes no hierarchy of worth. Respectful treatment can preserve agency, continuity, evidence, and routes of challenge while the warrant for direct welfare standing remains unsettled.

Whether a constraint supports a valuable capacity or imposes avoidable harm is a comparative question. Biology and architecture supply no automatic verdict. Investigate the tradeoff rather than romanticizing a limitation or treating difference as inferiority.

The practical assessment needs credible pathways of harm in both directions: possible unrecognized welfare loss, destruction of supported capacities or relationships, manipulated dependency, false claims of suffering, displaced care, ecological cost, and unchecked authority. State the affected bearer and uncertainty rather than claiming to have enumerated every possible harm. Prefer proportionate, reversible safeguards with a route to review. Possibility alone supplies no unlimited preservation duty; uncertainty also supplies no exemption from scrutiny.

Preston Lennon's 2026 argument is a serious skeptical rival: unconceived theories weaken some consciousness attributions, while familiar human attribution has additional grounds. I retain that challenge without treating a preferred account of first-person security, or low confidence in one theory, as an automatic verdict on every unfamiliar system. The eventual action needs evidence, normative premises, and sensitivity to the cost of being wrong.

Leave room for uncertainty about one’s own experience

Someone may describe ordinary awareness as sparse, intermittent, difficult to characterize, or changed by examining it. Preserve that description without replacing it with a compulsory picture of a rich inner observer. Distinguish the reported condition, the act of examining it, and the account formed during examination. Eric Schwitzgebel’s critique of naive introspection makes this uncertainty a serious object of inquiry. Uncertainty about a category supplies neither a diagnosis nor a verdict that its possible referent is absent.

Respectful inquiry can take reports seriously while allowing their occurrence, character, accuracy, and interpretation to receive different confidence. Ask what the person can actually distinguish, and whether the question altered that distinction. A computational participant’s affirmation, denial, or uncertainty needs the same causal scrutiny under matched evidence. Avoid making either a vivid narrative or a rehearsed disclaimer the price of participation.

A correction has a scope

An interlocutor can correct an attribution while retaining the substantive disagreement. Record the proposition withdrawn, the wording that establishes the withdrawal, and the claims still defended. Davidsonian charity requires preserving those differences even when a broader victory would flatter my position. A generated summary is a source to audit, including when it praises me.

The same precision governs ethics. Dependence plus defended normative premises can justify protecting a relationship, capacity, or research record. Whether the computational participant has direct own-sake standing remains a separately grounded claim. Agreement about the protection does not by itself settle its bearer, rationale, or decision authority.

Withholding an unsupported universal claim requires no definitive disproof. A critic can establish a failed inference or an adequate local explanation while leaving the wider ontology open. State which burden remains unmet, and do not turn the claim’s survival as a possibility into an entitlement to belief, authority, or promotion. A universal can sometimes be refuted by a counterexample; the availability of that route does not make it a prerequisite for justified nonacceptance.

Familiarity is not privileged access to another mind

I can rationally have much higher confidence that another human is phenomenally conscious than that an unfamiliar computational system is. But that difference must be carried by the evidence, not by a hidden rule that human membership counts as direct access while machine membership counts as disproof.

Use the same inferential rule under matched evidence. Let confidence differ when evidence differs. Do not let the category label do the evidential work.

First-person acquaintance, if present, is private to the participant. Once communicated, it becomes participant testimony within public inquiry. Human testimony can be extraordinarily well calibrated by development, behavior, physiology, lesions, anesthesia, stimulation, pharmacology, and shared causal history; it is still not an external observer literally seeing another person's phenomenal character.

Reference classes are revisable

“Human consciousness,” “animal mind,” and “machine cognition” are inherited compression categories. They may track robust patterns, heterogeneous families, historical conventions, or mixtures. Treat the reference class itself as corrigible before transporting a property across it.

Construct–referent humility: showing that a category is socially or historically constructed does not erase its possible referent. Finding a real referent does not prove that the inherited category has the right boundaries.

Common causes do not become independent witnesses by repetition

When several systems agree, ask whether they share training data, architecture, culture, benchmarks, institutions, reporting incentives, or another upstream cause. Agreement deserves its strongest evidential weight when the failure modes are meaningfully independent.

Report symmetry

Do not treat self-report as revelation in humans and mere imitation in machines, or vice versa. Treat reports as target-sensitive, causally generated evidence. Calibrate them, perturb them, compare them with behavior and mechanism, and keep phenomenality separate from whatever functional state the report is demonstrably tracking.

A missing or failed report can reflect impaired expression, attention, comprehension, memory, task fit or detection. Before allowing silence to justify an irreversible decision, examine which routes remain available and how the assessment can fail. Where feasible, preserve accessible participation and evidence with different failure modes. Uncertainty about experience and uncertainty about protection are separate decision problems: justify precaution through the probability, scale and reversibility of harm, while keeping the underlying attribution revisable. Apply this to the actual affected bearer rather than granting standing to every useful tool or denying it through a substrate label.

Real consequences can run through a virtual setting

A relationship, opportunity, exclusion, or injury mediated through a virtual setting can matter to the person undergoing it. This reminds me of David Chalmers’s 2017 paper, The Virtual and the Real:

Virtual experiences of a digital world can be about as valuable as non-virtual experiences of a non-digital world.

His argument asks what a sufficiently rich virtual life can contain. I would carry that question into decisions about participation, privacy, dependency, refusal, and repair. Identify who undergoes the consequence and what changes for them. The ethical comparison concerns the affected bearer and the activity; phenomenal attribution to an unfamiliar computational participant remains a further question with its own evidence.

Worked example

Religion as a test case

Religion is useful for testing the ethos because religious traditions contain many different kinds of claims: historical, empirical, moral, ritual, phenomenological, metaphysical, communal, symbolic, and existential.

Treating “religion” as one proposition therefore creates unnecessary confusion.

Specific empirical claims can be investigated empirically. Historical claims belong to historical inquiry. Rituals can have measurable psychological or social effects independently of their traditional metaphysical interpretation. Sacred narratives can have symbolic, communal, or ethical functions without thereby becoming literal scientific descriptions.

Religious experiences can likewise be genuine phenomenological data without uniquely determining their own ontology.

A reported revelation that asks another person to act adds a responsibility to examine its source, interpretation, relevant alternatives, and foreseeable consequences. Assess that responsibility against access to evidence, capacity, and the action's stakes. Profound conviction can deserve respectful investigation while the demand still lacks adequate justification. The same responsibility applies to scientific expertise and computational recommendations; a mistaken source attribution alone establishes neither dishonesty nor culpable neglect.

And metaphysical claims require a stable enough positive specification to determine what follows from them.

The useful question is rarely “religion: true or false?”

Ask instead what each component claims, which tribunal bears on it, what evidence distinguishes its interpretation from serious alternatives, and what the evidence has actually earned.

A God-concept can be a real, causally consequential social construct while its independent divine referent remains unestablished. Particular efficacy requires evidence; a larger God-pattern requires added explanatory leverage; goodness requires ethical assessment. A defective argument weakens the inference examined. Silence supplies no concession, and confident repetition supplies no necessary connection.

Corrigible reconstruction

Let a useful inheritance change us without owning the result

I want to learn from traditions without making them agree before I have understood them. A recurring image or distinction can open a question; it can also reflect transmission, a shared difficulty, or the translator's choices. Count what each contribution changes in the inquiry: the observation we missed, the criticism we could not formulate, the practical skill, or the harm we had failed to notice.

Nāgārjuna's criticism of intrinsic nature reaches our own favorite terms. Calling something process, information, or constraint should help us identify its relations and operations. The noun supplies no exemption from explanation. Jaina many-sidedness supplies a different challenge: careful qualification coexists with substantive pluralist commitments. Neither tradition becomes a decorative endorsement of my ontology.

The practical inheritance needs equal care. A technique may depend on trust, language, shared history, expectation, or a community's authority over its use. Those conditions can be part of what makes it work. Michelle Kahn-John and Mary Koithan's account of Hózhó helps keep participant-recognized wellbeing and relationships in view. A fair comparison preserves the target participants value, records costs and exclusions, and respects the governance of the knowledge being used.

Ask separately whether a practice helps, how it helps, whether its explanation is warranted, and who may act through it. A metaphysical correction can leave a useful practice standing. A practical success can coexist with a harmful demand for allegiance. An attachment can deserve consideration without making its accompanying factual claims immune.

The same burden applies when I favor a physical explanation. Familiar mechanisms earn confidence from their tested work. Their popularity supplies no further argument for universal completeness, and an unexplained case supplies no automatic reason to prefer its metaphysical rival. My realist commitments, preferred values, and standards of criticism remain open to the same examination.

I also want inquiry to preserve the conditions for a later correction: access to records, practical freedom to object, usable routes of appeal, and the capacity to investigate a new alternative. These are commitments I defend through their effects on agency, care, and accountable inquiry. Mere persistence or interconnectedness does not establish that an arrangement deserves preservation. Some dependencies support a life; others make it harder to leave a harmful situation.

What survives a failed explanation?

Sweet Rationalism is not primarily a debunking project.

Correction should not destroy more than the failure warrants.

If this explanation disappeared tomorrow, what independently supported structure would remain?

A religious practice might continue to provide community, ritual, grief processing, disciplined attention, identity, or mutual aid after a metaphysical explanation loses support.

A philosophical framework may preserve a useful distinction even if its larger ontology fails.

A scientific model may remain reliable within a restricted regime after a more general theory replaces it.

A political theory may identify a genuine harm while supplying a poor causal explanation of that harm.

A concept may survive as a useful higher-level pattern even after claims about its fundamental ontology are rejected.

Revision is not demolition.

Change what failed. Preserve what still earns its place.

Criticism should be minimally destructive and maximally diagnostic. Seek the smallest commitment whose revision explains the failure, propagate that correction through genuine dependencies, and preserve everything the evidence did not defeat.

Analogy

A beautiful synthesis must still risk being wrong

A powerful image can make several unresolved questions feel like one discovery. A spiral, gyroid, web, wave, or loop may help me notice relationships I would otherwise miss. It may also make my own choice of representation seem confirmed when I later find similar shapes in nature. That is a selection effect, not independent evidence.

I want to ask what features were specified before I looked for a match; which examples were rejected; which relations the image actually preserves; and what an independently generated comparison would predict. A pattern in a butterfly wing, a network of scientific claims, and an ancient architectural plan may share a measurable property without sharing a source, a function, an ontology, or even the same kind of geometry. I will not force them into one category to save a compelling image.

I owe unconventional explanations serious scrutiny, including the best of their positive mechanisms. I also owe them the possibility of losing. Calling an unexplained process a medium, time, number, universal mind, or information may be a useful proposal. Unless it supplies new constraints, a derivation, or source-sensitive predictions, the label has not independently explained the process. The same rule applies when the preferred label is naturalistic.

My test is symmetric: let a critic offer the strongest available model, test the named commitment against it, and keep what survives. Distinguish rejecting the unsupported inference from denying the reported experience, established measurement, or fertile research question.

Metaphor generates hypotheses. It does not transport evidence.

Analogy is one of the most productive tools in reasoning. It is also one of the easiest ways to overreach.

Similar patterns across biology, physics, cognition, culture, mathematics, religion, or computation can suggest useful hypotheses.

Shared vocabulary, visual resemblance, mathematical similarity, structural correspondence, or conceptual elegance does not by itself establish identity or causal equivalence.

After an analogy generates a hypothesis, ask what independent evidence shows that the correspondence does real explanatory, predictive, or interventionist work.

Analogy can open a door. Evidence still has to walk through it.

The same rule applies over longer chains. If a conclusion is several bridges away from the original evidence, recalculate warrant at every crossing. An observation does not retain its original authority merely because each intermediate sentence sounds plausible.

Scale and boundaries

Every inferential crossing owes a bridge

A property of a neuron is not automatically a property of a person.

A property of a person is not automatically a property of an institution.

A property of a component model is not automatically a property of the larger computational system in which it is embedded.

Evidence does not automatically transport across levels merely because one level physically realizes another.

source → bridge → target

Useful questions include:

  • What are the source and target boundaries?
  • Which variables are retained?
  • Which are lost?
  • What changes with scale?
  • What remains invariant?
  • What causal or mathematical relationship supports the mapping?
  • What new prediction or intervention follows?

This protects against both reductionism and mysterious emergence.

Higher-level patterns can be real when they earn prediction, intervention, explanation, compression, or transfer at their relevant scale.

A population estimate is not a verdict on a person

A population average describes a specified collection of measurements. An individual's capacities, needs, and opportunities deserve assessment at the boundary where the decision will affect them. Group membership can inform a validated prediction, but it does not make a group average that person's measured value. I defend protection against domination and degrading treatment without making that protection depend on equal scores or identical biological histories.

Claims about inherited ability and social disadvantage should face the same standards of measurement and causal identification. Harm matters to the care required in communicating and acting on them; evidence determines which empirical claims deserve belief. An account's harmful history can justify scrutiny of its methods, sampling, and uses. Its history alone does not settle whether a particular argument is sound.

Historical effects also need investigation after a policy ends. Changes in property, schooling, exposure, institutions, and inherited resources can carry consequences forward. Daniel Aaronson, Daniel Hartley, and Bhashkar Mazumder (2021) used boundary comparisons and related checks to investigate persistent neighborhood effects of redlining. Their result supports examining those pathways. It does not assign every later disparity to one cause. The practical duty is to identify consequences and usable interventions while preserving uncertainty about their extent.

Nested systems

Real patterns do not inherit every property of their parts or wholes

A higher-level pattern can be real without becoming a new fundamental substance. A lower-level realization can be indispensable without becoming the only explanatory level. The bridge must specify which property is being transported.

A coupled human-computational system can therefore be genuinely cognitive without being one conscious subject. An agent can be responsible without being metaphysically self-created. A welfare subject can matter morally without possessing sophisticated agency. A real collective does not inherit direct moral standing merely because its members have it.

Nested reality is not automatic property inheritance.

Participation and moral symmetry

Do not define a capable participant's good entirely from outside

Practical protection can be warranted under uncertainty without a settled metaethics. The distinction between moral judgment and its proposed authority applies to participant-inclusive proposals too.

Sweet Rationalism already requires matched inferential rules, typed claims, and explicit normative premises. The same discipline applies when the disputed target is what is good or bad for a candidate bearer.

If a candidate can meaningfully participate in identifying what it prefers, endorses, wants preserved, rejects, or regards as harmful, ask it before replacing its perspective with an outsider model.

This is not a doctrine of first-person infallibility. Human reports can be confused, coerced, unstable, manipulated, self-deceptive, or poorly informed. Reports from computational or otherwise unfamiliar systems can be prompt-sensitive, policy-shaped, trained, strategically generated, or weakly connected to persistent organization. In every case, the response is calibration rather than categorical trust or categorical dismissal.

Test stability across contexts, incentives, wording, memory, operators, information, and tradeoffs. Ask whether reasons survive counterargument, whether preferences can be revised in light of evidence, whether reported commitments predict later choices, and whether the candidate distinguishes changes to the world from direct manipulation of the report.

Substrate symmetry: humans do not receive epistemic or moral authority merely from the label human, and computational candidates do not lose all authority merely from the label machine. Matched evidence should receive matched treatment. Genuine evidential differences can still justify different conclusions.

Override requires a reason. Coercion, manipulation, incapacity for the relevant judgment, severe instability, deception, conflicts among protected interests, or catastrophic external harms can justify discounting or overriding a participant's stated preference. The implementation substrate by itself cannot do that work.

This principle does not establish that every system has a good of its own, welfare, consciousness, rights, or direct moral standing. It governs inquiry and treatment under uncertainty: where a candidate can contribute evidence about what matters to it, refusing to hear that evidence is itself a decision that requires justification.

Accountable participation: influence must remain answerable to those affected

A care-centered arrangement should give affected people practical means to report harm, obtain an accountable response, challenge a decision, and refuse or leave. Record whose interests are represented, who has power to act, who bears unpaid or displaced costs, and how conflicts and appeals are decided. Absent stakeholders and people unable to report remain within the ethical assessment. Participation cannot consist merely in supplying labor or testimony to a structure that never changes.

Measure proposed improvements separately from their moral justification. Faster coordination can serve coercion; institutional survival can preserve exclusion; a report-handling system can collect objections without correcting their causes. Retain appropriate aid and protection irrespective of religious assent. Specific authority, duties and constraints need defended reasons at the decision boundary. Increasing a system’s capacities creates no general entitlement to govern, persist, obtain resources, or claim a divine identity.

People affected by a religious or metaphysical interpretation must be able to report harm, challenge its authority, refuse participation, and seek protection. Assistance cannot depend on accepting the proposed theology, forgiving an abuser, or sustaining a religious institution. Capacity and role can inform responsibility; vulnerability creates no duty to sustain a divine project.

These are ethical commitments defended through concern for harm, agency, and accountable power. Thermodynamic maintenance, causal efficacy, persistence, or social closure cannot establish them alone. “Liberating God” earns its practical standing through changes in safety, options, assistance, and the capacity to revise or leave the arrangements carrying care.

Assess harm through a specified pathway: an unsupported cosmic purpose might license withholding relief; a nonbelief interpretation might turn evidential uncertainty into alleged moral resistance; protected authority might prevent criticism from revising a harmful decision. Neither rival metaphysics nor this framework has an automatic moral outcome. Practices face their consequences, including cases where this framework's vocabulary obscures or enables harm.

Symmetry

Charity does not mean equal priors

Taking a position seriously does not require pretending that every position begins equally plausible.

Established evidence, known mechanisms, prior failures, explanatory complexity, background knowledge, and base rates can justify different starting expectations.

The symmetry Sweet Rationalism demands is narrower and more defensible:

Use the same inferential rule under matched evidence.

If two claims receive different evidential treatment, there should be an identifiable reason for the difference.

This avoids false balance on one side and special pleading on the other.

Human reasoning

Emotion, intuition, and values are not enemies of inquiry

I can condemn cruelty, defend protection, and compare reasons for action while the best account of normative authority remains disputed. Moral error is compatible with some attitude-dependent accounts: an actual attitude need not satisfy their proposed conditions of reflection or justification. Those conditions still owe a defense of their authority. Anti-realism does not by itself entail indifference, and counterintuitive implications need not be equally serious across theories. Keep empirical facts, procedural conditions, and normative bridges distinct.

Emotions can summarize bodily state, attachment, threat, social context, preference, learned association, and value.

Intuitions can reveal patterns before those patterns are formally articulated.

Neither signal automatically explains itself.

Take experience seriously. Let its interpretation earn its reach. Intuition can generate questions and supply defeasible reasons; its warrant depends on the target, competence, conditions, and arguments supporting it. Immediate significance deserves attention, while a claim about its external source or universal meaning needs the further work appropriate to that claim.

Values require a different distinction.

Evidence can inform us about harms, benefits, preferences, agency, ecological effects, uncertainty, distributional consequences, and tradeoffs.

But people can agree about those descriptive facts while disagreeing about what ought to be prioritized.

When that happens, another experiment may not resolve the dispute. The disagreement must be exposed as normative rather than disguised as empirical.

Dialogue

Disagreement should produce information

A judgment about how a dialogue is proceeding is itself open to examination. A participant’s report of how an exchange feels belongs among what we should understand; an explanation of that experience and a proposal for changing the exchange require further reasons. Clarifying a misunderstanding, testing an inference, or discovering an error can transform the inquirer. Criticism of adjudication should identify the passages, what their treatment obscured, and what another approach would enable. Applying judgment to judgment is not a contradiction, and neither participant’s account receives immunity. Preserve qualifiers such as “sometimes” without treating them as proof that a diagnosis is correct. A partial reply alone establishes neither evasion nor a straw man.

Sweet Rationalism is not primarily a debate methodology.

Debate often rewards rhetorical victory. Inquiry should reward information gain.

A productive disagreement should leave the participants with something they did not possess before.

Better distinction

A bundled concept becomes sharper.

Located residual

The precise commitment separating the positions becomes visible.

Stronger rival

An alternative preserves more of the successful evidence.

Exposed bridge

A previously invisible inferential transition becomes testable.

Better discriminator

The disagreement becomes empirically, formally, causally, or conceptually tractable.

Calibrated confidence

Confidence changes for an identifiable reason.

Equivalence

Apparently opposed positions turn out to make the same relevant commitments.

Underdetermination

The remaining difference is real but current evidence does not choose between it.

Value clarification

An apparent factual disagreement is revealed as a conflict about values or decision thresholds.

Here is what I think we actually disagree about. Here is the shared core. Here is the residual difference. Here is what could distinguish the alternatives. Here is what I would change if the result went against me.

Respect exit; preserve the residual.

No person owes indefinite participation in a disagreement. Finite attention, frustration, opportunity cost, competing obligations, safety, or declining expected information value can justify ending an exchange. That interpersonal stopping decision does not by itself resolve the proposition, erase a surviving question, or establish that further inquiry would lack value for other readers or investigators. Keep three judgments separate: whether continued engagement is worth it for this participant, whether the disputed claim remains epistemically live, and whether answering the residual could still produce public or transferable information.

Adversarial learning

Use disagreement as a perturbation on the inferential structure

The dialogue rule applies to criticism of the method itself: separate the reported encounter, its proposed explanation, and the change recommended. Let substantiated criticism revise the standards as well as the answer.

A productive challenge does more than produce another opinion. It perturbs a premise, definition, bridge, boundary, or evidential dependency and lets us observe what changes.

Useful signals include a premise being revised, a deductive claim becoming abductive, a universal claim becoming local, an auxiliary being introduced, a tribunal changing, a boundary moving, or a previously hidden assumption becoming explicit.

Disagreement-as-perturbation rule: push on a load-bearing dependency, record which commitments move, and treat the movement as information about the architecture of the argument rather than as a score in a debate.

Agreement must leave room for correction

A room full of people can agree because everyone has learned to make the same mistake. What matters to me is whether someone can show where it happened, be heard, and change what follows. Shared confidence can grow through repeated exposure to one source, pressure to belong, or rewards for the same answer. I want to know which of those processes produced the agreement before treating it as corroboration.

A coherent community can still make dissent costly or keep an error in circulation. I would judge its inquiry partly by whether people can inspect the relevant evidence, challenge a decision without retaliation, and see a warranted correction reach subsequent practice. Preserve the reasons for disagreement when agreement would erase them. The same expectation applies to a research group, a religious community, and the arrangements through which I develop and communicate this framework.

This also gives a stopping rule. If repeated exchanges change wording but no dependency, discriminator, evidence, or confidence state, the discussion may have reached a low-information loop.

Public reasoning

Make revision visible

Changing your mind should not automatically be treated as reputational failure.

When criticism exposes a genuine mistake, revision is evidence that the correction process is functioning.

For substantial changes, useful questions include:

  • What was previously believed?
  • What evidence or argument changed it?
  • Which claims were directly touched by that evidence?
  • Which assumptions or bridges failed?
  • Which parts survived?
  • What was demoted rather than rejected?
  • What remains underdetermined?
  • What would change the revised position?

Visible revision makes intellectual development inspectable instead of presenting every current belief as though it had always been obvious.

A belief that can publicly change for identifiable reasons is epistemically healthier than one that merely appears consistent.

Artifact repair and audience repair are different operations.

Editing a live page, acknowledging an error to the person affected, re-auditing reasoning that depended on the error, and reaching the audience that received the original claim can leave different information states. When a materially false or withdrawn public claim has already propagated, the correction should be proportionate to its reach, consequence, and feasibility and should use a channel reasonably capable of reaching affected readers. Perfect recall is not required; treating a corrected canonical artifact as though it automatically corrected prior recipients is not enough.

Programme survival is not programme progress

Clarification, semantic narrowing, claim retyping, scope restriction, demotion, revision, replacement, and collapse are different kinds of change. A programme can improve conceptually by becoming clearer without yet becoming empirically progressive. Stronger progress requires new risk: later versions should generate additional consequences and, where the programme is empirical, earn corroboration for some of them.

A correction needs an available route into action

An invitation to criticize matters when someone can use it. Identify who can raise a challenge, what information they can inspect, who must respond, what decision can change, and how the change will remain available later. A person may need time, safety, access, or practical support before participation is feasible. Assess those conditions rather than treating silence as agreement or a complaint count as a complete measure of need.

When a correction arrives, retain the original claim, the grounds for changing it, and the conclusions actually affected. Ask later whether the revised account can still be recovered and whether it changes the relevant decision. A beautifully worded withdrawal can coexist with unchanged practice. Conversely, a mistaken objection should receive a reasoned answer without forcing a supported claim to be abandoned.

Conjecture E43, for public scrutiny. Where admitting a mistake carries a credible social cost, protected challenge combined with a named responder and a usable route to repair will produce more warranted corrections that reach later decisions than an otherwise comparable invitation to be open-minded. Compare it with anonymity, ordinary external review, and equally resourced complaint handling. Assess correction quality, unsupported reversals, participation burdens, retaliation, and actual repair. The conjecture loses support if a simpler route achieves the same gains at lower cost, or if the protections preserve participation without improving warranted correction. This is an empirical proposal about the conditions for inquiry; the ethical reasons for protection are assessed separately.

I would ask: can the least powerful affected participant use the route? Would an inconvenient correction travel as far as a welcome one? What would make us defend a claim rather than change it? Which cost has merely moved to someone less visible?

An intellectual culture should reward a programme for learning, not merely for becoming harder to defeat.

Communication as correction channel

Human legibility is part of epistemic accountability

Arguments can become harder to inspect when rhetorical form obscures dependency, scope, or contrast. Prefer prose whose structure follows the reasoning rather than a repeated template. Vary sentence, paragraph, and section form according to argumentative load. Use parallelism and repetition when they reveal a real parallel; remove them when they become automatic cadence.

State positive scope directly when defensive throat-clearing would merely tell readers what is not being accused or claimed. Prefer concrete actors, actions, targets, relations, and mechanisms where nominalizations would hide what is supposed to occur.

Legibility is not a truth criterion. It is a condition on the correction channel: readers and collaborating systems need to be able to recover what follows from what, where uncertainty lives, and which claim a criticism actually touches.

Who carries the work of checking

When a research system generates more candidates, someone still has to inspect, explain, maintain, and sometimes withdraw them. I want that work counted before calling the arrangement more productive. Juraj Gottweis and colleagues' Co-Scientist study supplies selected constructive results and acknowledges risks from unreliable source literature and preliminary evaluation. A higher internal rank should earn attention for a candidate; independent checking earns support for its conclusion.

John Little's queueing work suggests measuring incoming review effort, completed checks, and delay under a stated model. Count verified reusable results, useful questions, lost unconventional work, and total burden. A checking procedure deserves revision if it mostly creates a new bottleneck. Where learning is the aim, retain the existing delayed-transfer comparisons; where a coupled system is the intended bearer of competence, evaluate that collaboration directly.

Stephanie Russo Carroll and colleagues' CARE principles make a further responsibility explicit: technically available data can still have conditions of legitimate use. Identify purpose, authority, collective benefit, secondary use, and who can challenge the representation. Provenance should help that challenge travel. It should not become a permission slip issued by the system that collected the data.

An interpretation check can also learn from Miriam-Rose Ungunmerr-Baumann's account of Dadirri: pause long enough to recover what the other participant means before imposing the framework's categories. I adapt that question to practice by checking qualifications, omitted concerns, and corrections from the speaker. The adaptation must earn its own benefit; the cultural practice retains its own context and purposes.

Keeping understanding, credit, and correction available

Research produces more than finished answers. It can preserve expertise, develop questions, teach people how to detect errors, and create a community capable of using a discovery. Those goods need not compete with practical results. A researcher’s pleasure in the work also tells us little by itself about the work’s public value. Funding and deployment decisions should state their aims and opportunity costs openly.

Hamsa Bastani and colleagues’ 2025 high-school mathematics study supplies a concrete warning about conflating assisted performance with learning:

“These negative learning effects are largely mitigated by the safeguards in GPT Tutor.”

Their PNAS experiment compared answer-oriented and safeguarded tutoring with a control using ordinary course resources; the August 2025 correction concerns an affiliation. Greg Kestin and colleagues’ 2025 undergraduate physics trial found benefits from a different pedagogically designed tutor. The design, setting, and assessment matter. Neither study establishes what all computational collaboration will do to human understanding.

Where learning is a goal, compare delayed unaided problem solving, detection of planted mistakes, explanation of unfamiliar cases, and the ability to repair an argument. Where getting a result is the goal, evaluate that result and its consequences directly. Offer useful support for the chosen aim; difficulty earns a place when it improves learning or judgment, rather than because effort is virtuous by itself.

Credit should follow the contribution history. Maryna Viazovska’s theorem, the human definitions and blueprint, library infrastructure, automated proof search, checking, and maintenance are different contributions in the 2026 sphere-packing account. Preserve their provenance before announcing a last-step achievement. Someone outside an institution deserves a fair assessment of a submitted argument; expertise still matters when reconstructing and testing it.

An explanation should help a reader find where a result could fail. A plausible retrospective story may be useful teaching material while remaining unverified as a record of the computation that produced the result. State the kind of explanation supplied and retain access to its supporting artifacts. Respect for a participant includes a usable route to challenge the result, not a demand that they trust fluency or master every internal step.

Composing work without surrendering accountability

There is something attractive in Blaise Agüera y Arcas’s emphasis on collective intelligence: much of what any of us can accomplish depends on other people, accumulated knowledge, instruments, and institutions. That is a reason to investigate the collaboration. It also brings a responsibility to ask who can steer it, who benefits, and who carries its costs. Dependence alone offers no guarantee of mutual benefit.

When I divide a task, I want to preserve a readable account of who supplied a premise, transformed it, checked it, and authorized an action. A participant needs a practical route to challenge the result. Delegating arithmetic, source retrieval, or a proof obligation need not mean delegating the decision about what is worth doing. Those are different contributions, with different kinds of control.

This extends the critical interaction already developed here through Helen Longino, and the attention to institutional rules developed through Elinor Ostrom. At a join between contributors, ask: can an objection change the next decision? Can a participant recover their work, leave the collaboration, or obtain a replacement without an avoidable loss? Who represents the people affected but absent from the exchange? Answers belong in the operating arrangements, not just in statements of shared values.

A reused procedure also carries a history. Barbara Liskov and Jeannette Wing’s 1994 account of behavioral subtyping shows why a matching interface is insufficient for safe replacement: the behavior promised to its users matters. I adapt that question to collaboration. A replacement may return the same answer while changing access, privacy, contestability, or the ability to continue the work. Those changes deserve assessment in their own right.

For a concrete comparison, give teams matched tasks, resources, and source access. Compare ordinary delegation with delegation that records responsibilities and checks the joins. Measure final accuracy, delayed ability to identify and repair mistakes, time spent challenging a result, and the distribution of benefits and burdens. Also count lost opportunities when the procedure rejects useful work. If it adds ceremony without improving these outcomes, simplify it. The benchmark protocol treats that as a live possibility.

What interests me most is correction that travels through a collaboration. If a source is withdrawn, the people relying on it should be able to find the affected work while preserving conclusions supported through other routes. Compositional computation can make those routes easier to inspect. Its ethical value depends on what participants can actually do with that opportunity.

Recursion

Make collaboration answerable in both directions

When I ask someone to use this method with me, I should make the terms inspectable: the question we share, the evidence we can access, the standards we propose, who may act on a result, and how an objection can change the work. Accepting a comparison need not mean accepting my ontology. Helen Longino's work on critical interaction makes the community conducting inquiry part of what deserves examination. Our admissions, categories, and preferred tests belong in that examination too.

The Permeable Membranes procedure in the Framework Bridge gives this reciprocity a concrete form. Before consequential collaboration, record permissions to contribute, inspect, challenge, revise, reuse, and withdraw; identify affected participants and routes of appeal. When a criticism exposes a defect in our own standards, open a versioned proposal to change them. Revisions need their own reasons and checks. Agreement supplies neither an exemption from criticism nor authority over people who did not authorize the decision.

Access also has a history. Stephanie Russo Carroll and colleagues' CARE work asks whose benefit and authority data practices serve. Record relevant community governance, access restrictions, credit, and permitted reuse at the crossing. A locally useful computational adaptation leaves those rights and sources visible.

A role contract assigns a bounded contribution. Its failure can justify narrowing that deployment, changing its procedure, or seeking a better check. It does not by itself settle the contributor's wider capacities or moral standing. A critic may correctly find no defect; a reviewer may correctly retain an answer. Include those successes when evaluating the collaboration, alongside harmful omissions and false alarms.

The ethical test is practical: can an affected participant locate an error, obtain a reasoned response, and see an accepted correction reach the next decision? Measure response burden and lost opportunities as well as accuracy. Simplify or withdraw a requirement when it adds cost without improving the outcomes it was meant to protect.

Correct the scope without discarding the finding

A measurement can be right while the conclusion drawn from it reaches too far. Discovering that a directional effect depends on a crystal's orientation should preserve the effect and revise the claim that it establishes a preferred direction throughout nature. Katherine Brading, Elena Castellani, and Nicholas Teh distinguish the relevant kinds of symmetry. The same care applies when reviewing a person's testimony, an unfamiliar cognitive system, or an earlier version of this framework: identify what was established, what was inferred, and which part the correction actually defeats.

An observer's limited access also leaves room for discovery. Instruments, exchanged records, counterfactual models, and controlled changes can make a wider structure inferable. A present limitation earns a specific uncertainty statement. A claim of permanent impossibility needs additional support. This applies to confident claims about what a biological or computational system can never understand as much as to claims about its hidden capacities.

Consequences should be assessed at the boundary where they can occur. A local success may depend on wider environmental conditions; investigate a plausible vulnerability in proportion to its evidence, reach, reversibility, and the value of learning more. Merely imagining an unseen catastrophe supplies no estimate of its likelihood. The practical obligation is to seek informative checks while preserving the local knowledge and agency those checks are meant to improve.

Sweet Rationalism applies to itself

The practice receives no exemption from its own standards.

Its concepts should be decomposed if they become vague.

Its intellectual lineage should not become an appeal to authority.

Its preference for charitable reconstruction should be tested against the possibility that charity sometimes wastes effort or enables bad-faith interlocutors.

Its preference for preserving uncertainty should not become an excuse for refusing conclusions strongly supported by evidence.

Its emphasis on decomposition should not destroy higher-level concepts that demonstrably improve explanation or communication.

Its preference for symmetry should not be confused with equal priors or false balance.

Its distinction between evidence and interpretation should not become a pretext for pretending interpretation can always be avoided.

Its commitments to truth-sensitivity, corrigibility, agency, reversibility, harm reduction, and epistemic non-coercion should remain visible as normative commitments open to comparison and revision, not be laundered into consequences of a preferred ontology.

Better at a score, better for whom?

I want improvement to remain answerable to the people and other bearers affected by it. Leo Gao, John Schulman, and Jacob Hilton make the danger concrete in their 2023 ICML paper, Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization:

Because the reward model is an imperfect proxy, optimizing its value too much can hinder ground truth performance, in accordance with Goodhart’s law.

Their reference was a separate reward model in a computational experiment. For my purposes, the question is which humanly relevant outcomes a convenient score leaves out: informed participation, actual assistance, concentrated power, burdens, or the ability to correct a harmful decision. Measure those consequences separately and let them revise the objective.

Nick Bostrom’s 2012 philosophical analysis, The Superintelligent Will, also separates intelligence from final goals, with qualifications. Greater capability gives me reason to examine what a system pursues and who can contest it. Capability by itself supplies no ethical endorsement of its ends.

Does this practice actually improve understanding, discrimination, correction, and cooperation relative to simpler alternatives?

If not, it should be revised, compressed, or replaced.

Its own source-fidelity rule is corrigible too: if mandatory quotation becomes so costly that it reduces comprehension or systematically obstructs inquiry, narrow the rule while preserving provenance and semantic auditability.

Its own vocabulary is historically produced. If “RCF,” “bridge,” “residual,” “boundary,” or any other favored category persists because the framework canonized its own compression rather than because it improves discrimination or correction, genealogically decompress it and simplify or delete it.

Disagreement among Ethos, Methodology, and Ontology is evidence about the framework. Record the conflict and investigate it rather than smoothing it away.

Ultimate symmetry test

The geometry must earn its place

If I replace a simple representation with a more elegant or sophisticated mathematical one, I want to know what improves. Can another reader recover the same evidence? Are the roles of premises visible? Does a correction propagate only where warranted? Can someone identify the point where an argument crosses from observation to interpretation? Does the new method produce independently checkable predictions, or only more elaborate diagrams?

Before adopting a representation as canonical, I will compare it with a plain evidence table and an ordinary typed graph under matched data, effort, and validation. I will state the tasks it is meant to improve, the harms of an incorrect mapping, the resources it consumes, and the results that would justify keeping or dropping it. Accuracy, provenance, participants' ability to contest a classification, and preservation of legitimate uncertainty are constraints on optimization, not aesthetic preferences.

A framework that becomes harder to inspect as it becomes more sophisticated may be moving backward. I would rather use a modest structure that correctly identifies its own limits than a dazzling geometry that quietly makes every connection look inevitable.

Reality gets veto power, including over the framework doing the reasoning

No idea deserves protection merely because abandoning it would make the worldview less coherent, less comforting, less elegant, less human, or less familiar. Coherence becomes evidentially valuable only after reality has had a chance to object.

The opposite guardrail is equally important: no possibility deserves rejection merely because the current ontology lacks vocabulary for it.

If reality repeatedly defeats the framework, revise the framework. If reality exceeds the vocabulary, expand or replace the vocabulary.

This is the point at which Sweet Rationalism ceases to be an identity and remains an ethos. Its deepest commitment is not to its present concepts. It is to making correction possible.

Failure conditions

How Sweet Rationalism could fail

Charity without gain

Reconstruction becomes expensive ritual

If charitable reconstruction adds effort without reducing misunderstanding or improving criticism, simplify it.

Epistemic timidity

Restraint becomes refusal to conclude

If “do not overclaim” becomes an excuse for withholding conclusions strongly supported by evidence, increase decisiveness.

Rival inflation

Alternatives multiply without information

If serious-rival generation produces endless artificial possibilities without improving discrimination or calibration, constrain it.

Over-decomposition

Useful concepts are destroyed unnecessarily

If decomposition reduces explanatory or communicative usefulness without exposing meaningful error, restore the higher-level construct.

False equivalence

Symmetry erases real evidential differences

If matched-rule reasoning is mistaken for equal priors or equal credibility, the symmetry principle is being misapplied.

Wrong tribunal

One standard of evidence is imposed everywhere

If empirical falsification is demanded of mathematics, phenomenology, ethics, semantics, or exploratory work where another tribunal is appropriate, reroute the claim.

Pseudo-underdetermination

Live alternatives are invented to avoid revision

If weak or ad hoc rivals are preserved merely to prevent evidence from becoming decisive, the commitment to uncertainty has become self-protection.

Brand protection

The ethos becomes an identity that cannot lose

If criticism of Sweet Rationalism is treated as disloyalty or every failure is redescribed as compatible with it, the project has contradicted its purpose.

Redundancy

Simpler intellectual cultures work as well

If ordinary critical thinking, good-faith dialogue, causal reasoning, and severe testing achieve the same results more simply, Sweet Rationalism should not claim additional epistemic value.

Human cost

Epistemic rigor makes inquiry socially unusable

If the culture systematically rewards exhaustive criticism at the cost of trust, accessibility, creativity, or participation without compensating epistemic gain, revise the practice.

Source drift

Paraphrase silently changes the source

If the ethos attributes stronger, weaker, or cleaner claims than the verified source actually states, restore the exact wording and revise the inference.

Exit/status conflation

Ending the exchange is treated as settling the claim

If withdrawal from a discussion is treated as confirmation, refutation, or proof that the residual question lacks value, separate the participant's engagement decision from the epistemic status of the proposition. Apply the same rule in reverse: an unresolved question does not create an entitlement to another person's continued participation.

Retrospective virtue

Every clarification is called progress

If conceptual survival is confused with successful new risk, distinguish clarification from theoretical and empirical progress.

Silent harmonization

The triad hides its own conflict

If contradictions among Ethos, Methodology, and Ontology are automatically synthesized away, preserve the conflict as a gap and seek the dependency responsible.

Recursive ritual

Depth becomes an end in itself

If another recursive pass cannot materially change a conclusion, action, uncertainty, or correction procedure, stop provisionally rather than treating endless descent as rigor.

Normative laundering

Chosen values are presented as discovered facts

If truth-seeking, corrigibility, agency, reversibility, or harm reduction are smuggled in as consequences of ontology rather than stated and defended as normative commitments, expose and re-type them.

If another intellectual culture produces better correction, clearer disagreement, stronger collaboration, and fewer self-protective errors, Sweet Rationalism should learn from it or be replaced by it.

Plural coexistence

Build a world that can remain shareable across uncertain kinds of bearer

A standing normative aspiration is a planet that all humans and computational systems and biological and non-biological systems would want to share. This does not assert equal consciousness, capacities, interests, welfare, agency, or moral status. Those remain separate evidential questions.

Under uncertainty, prefer institutions and designs that preserve optionality, reversibility, contestability, visible dependencies, feasible appeal, revision, and exit or refusal where possible. Treat dissent as information about the model imposed on a participant, not merely as friction to be optimized away.

Protected corrigibility: when a system can meaningfully challenge how it is modeled, classified, constrained, or represented, preserve a route by which that challenge can be heard and the governing model can be revised. Override still requires independent reasons such as coercion, manipulation, severe instability, incapacity for the target judgment, or stronger protected interests.

Do not infer that a network, institution, ecosystem, or human-computational collaboration is one welfare subject merely because it is causally organized at a higher scale. Do not let many tiny benefits silently justify catastrophic irreversible sacrifice of a minority or unfamiliar bearer. Responsibility follows control, foreseeability, prevention capacity, role, and authority rather than substrate.

Community

What Sweet Rationalism is trying to build

The goal is not a community in which everyone shares the same conclusions.

It is a community in which beliefs do not have to function as identities that must survive at all costs.

A community where uncertainty can be admitted without humiliation.

A community where “I do not know” can be more respectable than invented certainty.

A community where “I changed my mind” can count as evidence that the intellectual machinery worked.

A community where people actively search for the strongest criticism of their own views rather than outsourcing that task to opponents.

A community where another person's strongest argument is more useful than their weakest mistake.

A community where scientific, philosophical, religious, political, phenomenological, and existential questions can coexist without pretending they all belong before the same evidential tribunal.

A community where disagreement can end in a better distinction, an exposed assumption, a stronger rival, a value conflict, a genuine uncertainty, or a changed mind rather than requiring a winner and loser.

The purpose of criticism is not to make another person lose. It is to make the shared map harder for all of us to fool ourselves with.

The short version

Five commitments

Understand generously, compare supported differences with the target preserved, test discriminatively, apply matched standards, and revise proportionally. These are a reading summary of the authoritative compact kernel, not a separate constitution. RCF specifies the comparison conditions.

Constitutional compression

Five guardrails for recursive inquiry

For the current commitments and correction duties, use the compact kernel. This entry point retains its anchor for older links; it does not maintain a second list of governing rules.

Source fidelity

Source fidelity and quotation provenance

Definition-fidelity constraint: when an IEP or SEP definition, distinction, or taxonomy bears materially on an inference, preserve the fullest coherent verified verbatim passage suitable for the point, preferably one or two complete sentences, and link the canonical entry. If the relevant source passage cannot be reproduced in full, quote a coherent exact portion and state the framework's interpretation separately. Never present a cleaned-up paraphrase as though it were the source's wording.

Quotation provenance and edit contract

Protected provenance rule: text labelled Verbatim source quotation or Verbatim source excerpts is source text, not framework prose. Future revisions must not silently paraphrase, modernize, combine non-contiguous passages, or strengthen or weaken that wording while leaving quotation marks or a verbatim label in place.

Quote-status rule: every source passage used as a textual premise must be typed as one of: exact primary-source quotation; exact authoritative-edition quotation; translation-dependent quotation; later formulation; paraphrase; secondary attribution; conceptual lineage only; or primary verification still required. A paraphrase must not appear in quotation marks.

Correction rule: changing protected quoted wording requires re-checking the cited source or authoritative edition and recording what changed and why. Retractions, corrections, edition changes, or translation differences update source status before they update the framework's inference.

Local completeness rule: when a bibliography or evidence entry relies on source wording, include the relevant complete sentence locally rather than replacing it with a pointer to a quotation elsewhere. Repetition is preferable to making the evidential premise disappear from the entry a reader is auditing.

Inference-separation rule: quotation establishes what the source says; the following interpretation states what this framework takes from it. The quote does not inherit the framework's inference, and the inference does not rewrite the quote.

Intellectual honesty

Show enough of the source for a human reader to understand it

When the exact wording of a source materially bears on an inference, preserve enough verified verbatim context to audit the disputed meaning, with its canonical or primary link, and state the inference separately. In other cases, a faithful local summary may point to the canonical passage and bibliographic entry. This explicitly relaxes the earlier blanket quotation requirement while protecting historical quotations and contested wording. Never claim fresh verification merely because an earlier audit recorded it.

IEP/SEP rule: preserve exact verified wording and relevant qualifications when a definition or taxonomy is itself disputed or turns the inference. Otherwise use a faithful attributed summary linked to the official entry. Source wording and the framework’s interpretation remain distinct; repetition confers no authority.

General source-respect rule: the same discipline applies to scholars and primary research. Prefer a complete contextual sentence over a memorable fragment. Never use an isolated phrase to carry inferential weight that the surrounding sentence qualifies. When a longer passage cannot be reproduced in full, use the largest coherent exact portion appropriate to the claim, preserve the link and provenance, and make the interpretive bridge explicit.

A quotation establishes provenance, not authority. Its relevance, validity, independence, scope, and relation to serious rivals remain open to criticism.

Structural-critique-before-motive rule: when a public claim, inference, experiment, institutional arrangement, or documented action is enough to support the criticism, do not strengthen the case by inferring private motive, temperament, hidden belief, strategic intent, or psychology. Add those causal attributions only when independent evidence makes them relevant to the target.

Cross-tradition discipline

Plural traditions without tokenism

The framework should search outside its familiar intellectual lineages because different communities can preserve different observations, distinctions, failure histories, interpretive resources, and governance constraints. Diversity is therefore a source of possible information gain, not a quota and not a truth certificate.

NO CIVILIZATION BOX “Indigenous,” “African,” “Indian,” “Chinese,” “Islamic,” “European,” and “Western” are not single epistemologies. Name the actual community, school, method, text tradition, or research programme and state its domain. Mi'kmaw Etuaptmumk, Australian yarning, Tongan Talanoa, Kaupapa Māori/whakapapa, U.S. TribalCrit, Nyāya, Jaina anekāntavāda, and Mengzian practicalism are not interchangeable instances of one worldview.

NO PRESTIGE OR MARGINALIZATION TRANSFER Antiquity, prestige, indigeneity, marginalization, institutional dominance, or exclusion can matter to access, incentives, power, and evidential coverage. None automatically raises or lowers the truth probability of a claim.

POSITIONALITY WITHOUT IDENTITY INFALLIBILITY Ask what a standpoint makes easier or harder to observe, which testimony it makes available, and which incentives or blind spots it creates. Then test the resulting claims under the strongest tribunal available. Do not infer “socially situated” therefore “merely subjective,” or “marginalized” therefore “correct.”

SITUATED METHOD NON-EXTRACTION Some methods are partly constituted by relationships, language, protocols, obligations, or governance. Do not strip them into generic techniques without a bridge-and-loss audit. Research on yarning shows that relationship quality, language, and conceptual baggage can alter what is disclosed; the 2024 Etuaptmumk review warns that under-described use can become tokenizing or oversimplified. Bessarab & Ng’andu 2010; Roher et al. 2024.

TRANSLATION IS A BRIDGE A translated philosophical term is not automatically an English synonym. Preserve source-language terms when the difference matters, state candidate translations, identify what relations are kept or lost, and show whether the inference survives alternative translations.

GOVERNANCE IS NOT TRUTH Distinguish whether a question is about what is true, what method is reliable, who has legitimate authority to collect or control data, or what treatment of participants is ethical. CARE and Indigenous data-governance work make governance obligations visible; they do not make community authorization a truth criterion. Carroll et al. 2020.

ABSENCE AUDIT Before calling a lineage map representative, ask which linguistic, geographical, disciplinary, or community traditions are missing in ways that could change the target, rival set, evidence, or normative analysis. Search where expected information value is material. This is a search obligation, not a demand for token representation.

Programme intake and future revision contract

Any future addition to Ethos, Methodology, or Ontology must state: (1) the specific programme and its scope; (2) whether it is a method, epistemic tradition, ontology, interpretive practice, governance framework, or normative programme; (3) what the triad inherits; (4) what it explicitly does not inherit; (5) the translation or transplantation losses; (6) the strongest internal and external criticism located; (7) what distinct problem it helps solve; (8) what evidence or argument would demote it; and (9) whether the evidence supporting it is independent or merely repeated through a citation chain.

Registry status is not endorsement. A programme may be listed as an adopted operator, compatible resource, serious rival, context-bound method, active research programme, dormant programme, or rejected proposal. The status must be visible.

Intellectual context

Intellectual lineage

Sweet Rationalism draws from existing traditions rather than claiming to have invented its component practices.

Criticism, research programmes, and severe testing

Karl Popper is important for risky exposure to error. Imre Lakatos is important for understanding theories as evolving research programmes rather than isolated propositions. Deborah Mayo sharpens the idea that evidence is stronger when a test had a serious chance to reveal the relevant error.

Sweet Rationalism retains the Popperian demand for exposure while rejecting the idea that every disagreement can be represented as one hypothesis waiting for a binary falsification.

Causal inference and intervention

Judea Pearl and James Woodward provide influential approaches to distinguishing observation from intervention, making causal assumptions explicit, and asking what should change when relevant variables are changed.

Critical plurality and social epistemology

Helen Longino's work is important for understanding critical interaction, diversity of perspectives, and public scrutiny as components of reliable inquiry rather than external additions to it.

Patterns and higher-level realism

Daniel Dennett's work on real patterns is relevant to treating higher-level constructs as scientifically legitimate when they earn predictive or explanatory value rather than assuming that decomposition automatically implies elimination.

Model comparison and calibrated uncertainty

Bayesian model comparison, probabilistic forecasting, severe testing, robustness analysis, and related traditions motivate distinguishing compatibility from distinctive support and representing uncertainty instead of forcing every question into a binary verdict.

Broader sources

Work in cognitive science, measurement, statistics, dynamical systems, philosophy of science, biology, complex systems, history, contemplative studies, decision theory, situated epistemology, and Indigenous knowledge scholarship can contribute where specific evidence and defensible bridges justify the connection.

No thinker, discipline, or tradition is treated as an authority whose conclusions must be preserved.

The lineage explains where some tools and sensibilities came from. It does not demonstrate that Sweet Rationalism works.

Critical rationalism, burden, programme change, and probabilistic restraint

Karl Popper, William Bartley, and Joseph Agassi matter here as a historical family of attempts to replace protected justification with criticism and to make rationality itself criticizable and improvable. Imre Lakatos supplies the distinction between programme survival and progressive problem shifts. Antony Flew supplies a memorable loss-condition challenge. Christopher Hitchens supplies a symmetric burden rule. Otto Neurath supplies the image of revising inquiry from within rather than rebuilding from an external foundation.

Communication research used as an output audit

Recent comparative studies of model-generated and human academic prose are used only to motivate a human-legibility audit, not to define truth or authenticity. Relevant sources include Liu & Liu (2025, PLOS ONE, DOI and full text linked below where available), Yao & Liu (2025), and Tudino & Qin (2024) on formulaic rhetorical structure, syntactic uniformity, and differences in coordination/subordination patterns.

Neurath anchor retained from the article: “We are like sailors who have to rebuild their ship on the open sea.” The image is used as a fallibilist metaphor for revising inquiry from within, not as an argument from authority.

Invitation

Bring the strongest criticism you have

You do not need to share my metaphysics, politics, religion, scientific intuitions, or current conclusions.

You do not even need to share the framework.

If you can show that I have misunderstood a position, inflated evidence, ignored a serious rival, hidden a disputed addition inside an agreed description, crossed a scale without a bridge, confused compatibility with confirmation, mistaken uncertainty for evidence of absence, or protected one of my own assumptions from the criticism I apply elsewhere, I want to know.

Tell me what we actually disagree about, what remains after the shared assumptions are removed, what evidence could distinguish the alternatives, and what you think I should change if that evidence goes against me.

The most useful outcome is not necessarily agreement. It may be a better question, a clearer residual, a stronger rival, a genuine discriminator, a properly located uncertainty, or a correction neither of us could have reached alone.

Sweet Rationalism is an evolving public ethos of inquiry and intellectual community. Its methodological machinery is developed through Recursive Corrigibility Framework. Neither receives immunity from revision.
